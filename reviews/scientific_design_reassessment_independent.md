**Assessment:** the live design can answer a meaningful but narrow question: can a prespecified guarded rule operate on genuinely prospective randomized pair exposures, retain incomplete episodes, and issue or withhold a decision under an auditable laboratory serving regime? It cannot establish routine success preservation near equal performance, a benefit from substantial asynchronous delay, or an operational advantage after switching traffic. ## 1. What root is responsible for

Root therefore needs to make and record three scientific decisions:

1. **Accept feasibility as the live objective at the available fixed horizon.** Do not require successful deployment to count as study completion or imply that the margin was independently elicited from an application owner. The manuscript already calls component margins illustrative.
2. **Accept or reject the measured serving-regime target explicitly.** Pair-shared GPU contention, the execution-lock transfer and the task exclusions are part of the observed preference. They are not removed by obtaining a low nominal error probability.
3. **Specify which exact adapter and comparison question the CPU study validates.** The choice between conservative live certificates and tighter feasible-set enumeration, the baseline event schedule, Monte Carlo alert interpretation and resource-only smoke amendment are methodological responsibilities. Root should adjudicate them prospectively rather than instructing the collector to make every comparison agree.

The bounded rule's theory, its implementation, empirical sensitivity and operational relevance are separate acceptance questions. Implementation repairs remain necessary, but a green test suite does not resolve an overly broad scientific claim. Conversely, a null, resource-limited or abstaining result does not show that a theorem failed.

## 2. Live horizon and success guardrail: informative limitation, uncalibrated power

With `alpha_gate=.00625`, `rho=100`, complete scores and margin `.03`, the success gate requires

`mean(D) > r(n)-.03`, where `r(n)=sqrt((n+100) log((n+100)/(100*.00625^2)))/n`.

Independent arithmetic gives:

| Complete enrolled pairs | Radius | Required observed success difference | Minimum net extra candidate successes |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 100 | .465693 | strictly above .435693 | 44 |
| 295, S1-only ceiling | .228707 | strictly above .198707 | 59 |
| 565, post-smoke ceiling before further exclusions | .158404 | strictly above .128404 | 73 |

The last column uses the integer score sum `sum(D)`; it is not a same-task count of improved tasks. The true horizon is the final eligible roster's pair count, which may be smaller (`protocol_FINAL.md:78,195–202`). Pending outcomes can only make the required observed evidence more conservative.

At an observed success difference of exactly zero, this rule first allows the guardrail at 17,097 complete pairs. At 100 all-positive success contrasts it deploys. Both statements are correct; neither supplies an actual deployment probability. In particular, the zero pilot mean and same-task pilot standard error do not calibrate a different-task, single-exposure, new-runtime, sequential experiment. The corrected live text appropriately withdraws that transfer (`:242–265`).

This is a poor design for **demonstrating a small success loss has been excluded when performance is nearly equal**, at this horizon and with this conservative bound. It remains useful for documenting the rule's abstention and possible response to sufficiently large observed effects. This review does not recommend increasing the horizon, loosening the margin, changing alpha/rho or selecting a different model after observations. A precise claim is “the rule did or did not certify this margin for the recorded running target at this prefix.” Do not convert abstention into equivalence, inferiority, empirical evidence of actual harm, or a generic impossibility for other methods.

The current eight CPU cells do not estimate live power. Their truths are fixed iid values and their delay/cost clocks are synthetic (`live_ab_validation/PROTOCOL.md:160–181,455–505,557–603`). They can diagnose coding and calibration behavior under those laws. A favorable-alternative cell with `mu_s=.20` is not evidence that this live success contrast has that effect or adequate near-zero sensitivity.

## 3. Pairing and metrics: a well-defined narrow target is not automatically useful replacement evidence

Balanced orientation identifies a symmetrized cross-arrival comparison under the frozen randomization assumptions. It does not identify ordinary same-task preference. Under partner-dependent resource use or success, it also does not identify a standalone per-task success effect: the relevant potential records are **pair-regime records** (`protocol_FINAL.md:1486–1530`). Keeping that modified target is statistically coherent if nonanticipation, boundedness and the actual observation rules hold. It answers how the two workflows compare when one of each runs together under this harness.

Three constraints materially affect usefulness:

- **Cost includes harness asymmetry.** The live latency endpoint charges self-test lock waiting to the repair workflow while its partner's hidden-test verification uses the same lock outside the partner's cost (`:1284–1293`). Within-pair contention and T3 cross-process sharing add further regime dependence (`:1273–1282,1295–1306`). A valid preference interval can therefore chiefly describe the accounting/scheduling regime. The planned S-lock and success-only readouts are valuable diagnostics; a sign change after removing recorded lock wait would demonstrate sensitivity to that measurement choice, not identify the causal benefit of removing the lock.
- **The serving regime changes after a switch.** After an all-one-arm traffic switch the comparator no longer shares each pair (`:1299–1305`). The historical running bound says nothing about performance in that new regime without further assumptions. A logged switch is evidence of laboratory execution, not validated operational savings.
- **Trial/task composition limits transport.** Reference-verification exclusions select the roster partly on resource requirements, tasks recur across trials, and T1/T2 reverse one workflow contrast after pilot-informed direction choices (`:99–174`). They are not independent replications or a fresh-task generalization study. Distinct assignments and machine histories can also give T1/T2 different realized running targets; they should not be treated as exact numerical negatives.

Root should judge metric usefulness from the deciding-tier shares, hidden-test success and failures, per-arm lock/timeout/unknown-usage records and already planned stratum/readout sensitivities, preserving the frozen primary endpoint. No new grading experiment is needed to make those interpretations honest. Internal self-test success is a workflow signal; final success remains the declared hidden-test endpoint. Root's separate saved-trajectory audit addresses their mismatch; this report does not independently reproduce those counts or infer a causal repair effect from them.

## 4. Partial information: the live schedule limits what can be demonstrated

The pair-synchronous live schedule allows at most one unresolved pair (`protocol_FINAL.md:99–125,849–867`). For a fixed enrolled prefix `n`, that pair's score interval has width at most 2. Relative to the hypothetical complete-data band **at the same prefix**, its contribution can inflate total band width by at most `2/n`, and can depress the lower endpoint by at most `2/n` (clipping may reduce either difference). These are distinct quantities, each with that bound; this is not a comparison to a different completed-prefix denominator.

After one episode's final success is revealed, the **success-difference** interval has width 1, so these same-prefix bounds improve to `1/n` for that score. The **hierarchy** may still have interval [-1,1] until latency information certifies more. At 565 pairs the two generic bounds are .003540 and .001770 respectively. This arithmetic limits the unresolved-score contribution; it does not bound calendar waiting time, since the remaining episode could still be slow.

Thus the trial can demonstrate genuine partial-state recording and perhaps a decision before its partner finishes, but it cannot establish the value of handling many pending pairs. The planned CPU panel is where informative delay is actually stressed. Moreover, the live current-full-prefix normal-mixture rule is not the manuscript's selected-prefix-envelope rule: it does not inherit that separate construction's no-later-than-completed-prefix result. Preserve the live protocol's explicit exclusion of substantial-delay and pathwise speed claims. This is a scientific design boundary, not a request to redesign scheduling on a shared GPU.
