## 1. The comparison contract asks different valid adapters to agree numerically

**Observed defect in the validation contract, not a demonstrated coverage failure of either adapter.** `vband.py:371–438` enumerates all feasible cost orderings. When a candidate is revealed successful at cost 10 and its incumbent partner is pending with elapsed lower bound 10 and cap 100, candidate loss is impossible but a tie is possible: the validation hierarchy interval is **[0,1]**. The pinned live `lab_enclosure.py:448–497` intentionally keeps **[-1,1]** until its strict sufficient win certificate holds. Both intervals contain every possible final score.

This state is reachable under the delivered cells: use atom `BB+`, final costs (10,40), the noninformative LONG branch with duration 100, first candidate reveal offset 0, and pending age 25; its prescribed elapsed cost is `40*25/100=10`. It is not an out-of-domain malformed input. With 100 identical such partial states the lower hierarchy bands differ by **0.534307079482**: validation gives `-.465692920518`, live gives `-1`. A second reproducible difference occurs at `ell=200/19`: validation returns [1,1], while the live certificate's extra `1e-9` conservatism keeps [-1,1]. This is not summation-order noise.

The preregistration nevertheless demands enclosure and band equality within `1e-12`, exact decisions and exact stopping prefixes (`PROTOCOL.md:1218–1226`), limits tolerance to summation order (`:1263`), calls every disagreement a defect (`:1268–1282`), and says the cost readings are known to coincide (`:1290–1293`). They do not coincide. Numerical failure of that comparison would not by itself identify an invalid confidence bound; the simulation of the tighter validation adapter would not automatically calibrate the actual live rule either.

**Repair before the grid:** adjudicate and freeze which concrete enclosure policy is the object under validation. For exact numerical validation of the live rule, the independent comparator must implement its declared conservative partial-enclosure policy, including the sufficient certificate and epsilon. Alternatively retain the tighter enumeration as a separately named valid construction, explicitly abandon equality as its correctness criterion, and validate the actual live construction separately. Do not silently alter the pinned live code, enlarge the equality tolerance or conceal the already found discrepancy. The existing disagreement/adjudication mechanism is suitable for recording this correction now.

The common cost-certificate flag explanation also needs precision. The live flag is a **pair-level** conjunction of hierarchy and success point intervals; a pending partner leaves the success interval open, so the live flag remains false. The validation hierarchy certificate is also false in that common pending state. `PROTOCOL.md:1251–1258` should not describe those states as automatically producing thousands of contradictory flags merely because one hierarchy interval is a point. Distinct definitions can still be excluded from direct comparison, but the stated example does not establish their disagreement.

## 2. The last-look reduction fails for the completed-data baselines

`PROTOCOL.md:829–838` and `cells.json`'s `grid.one_look_per_enrollment_prefix` claim exact preservation of all-look miscoverage and decision prefix by keeping only the final look at each enrolled prefix. This argument is correct for **ADAPTER**, because its denominator and radius stay fixed while the enclosure sums narrow. It does not establish the same property for **CPREFIX** or **NAIVE**: their effective index changes as more pairs complete, so their bands need not be nested at a fixed enrolled prefix.

An exact witness lies within the T4 horizon and the `L1/N` cell's support. All pairs have first reveal equal to full reveal, which has positive probability under the stated schedule:

| Pair positions | Final hierarchy and success scores | Full reveal tick |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1–500 | 0,0 | each enrollment tick |
| 501–600 | +1,+1 | 1,010 |
| 601–700 | -1,-1 | 1,100 |
| 701–1,000 | 0,0 | 1,200 |

The late offsets range from 200 to 509, all inside LONG's 100–699 support. Use `BB0`, `C>I` and `I>C` respectively. During the drain the enrolled prefix remains 1,000. At the enrollment cap, both baselines have completed index 500 and do not decide. At tick 1,010 their index is 600 and their score mean is `1/6`; `r(600)=.153363950255`, so both lower endpoints are **+.013302716411**, producing DEPLOY and excluding the true zero mean. At the finalization tick 1,200 all 1,000 scores average zero, the bands contain zero and neither condition crosses. Retaining only cap/finalization looks misses the entire baseline crossing and ever-miscoverage event. No stochastic panel was run to construct this counterexample.

**Repair:** preserve all distinct completion-index looks needed by CPREFIX/NAIVE, including the drain, while retaining the valid ADAPTER reduction. Alternatively explicitly define the baselines as coarsely scheduled rules and remove claims of equivalence to their all-trigger versions; report calibration for precisely that schedule. Also freeze atomic batching/tie order for simultaneous events and count any additionally evaluated looks honestly. This is a runner specification issue: `vgen.py` and `vrun.py` are not delivered yet, so there is no existing simulation output to invalidate or regenerate.

## 3. Smoke horizon and cell effects are confounded

`PROTOCOL.md:875–907` uses 10 programs in C1 at horizon 2,000 and 10 in C2 at horizon 1,000, then calls `log(s_2000/s_1000)/log(2)` a measured horizon-scaling exponent. The delay construction also changes between those points. Completing every path prevents early-stop selection but does not establish equal event-processing cost between delay mechanisms; “close to cell-independent” is not an identification argument.

A purely algebraic counterexample: if each cell's runtime scales linearly with horizon, but C1 costs twice as much per pair as C2, the proposed ratio is four and the reported exponent is two, despite a within-cell exponent of one. **Repair:** use the same prespecified cell at both horizons, or balance both cells over both horizons within the existing bounded smoke allowance; fix how estimates feed the budget ladder before timing is observed. No increased model or compute study is requested. The separate seed namespace, withholding effect columns and outcome-independent tier selection are good safeguards, conditional on implementation. Root owns the broader budget/smoke adjudication.

## 4. Monte Carlo flags are alerts, not deterministic proof of a defect

The Wilson formula and all six tabulated minimum flagging counts reproduce (`PROTOCOL.md:951–956,1009–1018`; `cells.json:527–540`). Trial-level rates use `4P` simulated trials and program-family rates use `P` programs; these are appropriate under the planned separately seeded independent trial streams. The three constructions share data and must not be counted as extra independent Monte Carlo replications. These are pointwise approximate 95% Monte Carlo intervals, not simultaneous coverage across every reported row.

Two interpretation corrections are needed:

- “A flag on ADAPTER is a defect” (`PROTOCOL.md:1020`, `cells.json:539`) is too strong. Even a valid procedure can produce a chance flag in finite Monte Carlo samples, and many correlated diagnostic rows are inspected. Call it **an exceedance alert requiring investigation**, retain and report it, and distinguish a deterministic counterexample or verified implementation defect from sampling variability. Multiplicity correction is not essential for descriptive alerts, but unadjusted alerts cannot become a certain family-wide defect verdict.
- The thresholds `64/8000` and `147/20000` are minimum **observed event fractions** that trigger this rule. They are not minimum detectable true violation rates or a power guarantee. `PROTOCOL.md:170–172` incorrectly says smaller true violations would pass unflagged. Report the critical counts and Monte Carlo precision; do not infer detection probability from them. The chosen budget tier must determine the actual denominators and critical counts, including the 1,000-program tiers.

The precommitted NAIVE positive-control check is a useful diagnostic and may remain a required acceptance condition. Its failure would leave the planned validation criterion unmet; it would not alone prove that the adapter's interval formula is wrong. Preserve all outcomes and follow the declared investigation/retention rule.
