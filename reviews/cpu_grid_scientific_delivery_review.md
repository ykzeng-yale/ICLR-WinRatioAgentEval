## Four scientific corrections remain open

### 1. The grid object is the tighter enclosure, not the deposited live policy

`vgen.state_at_age` uses the partial feasible-set sign `q` and permits intermediate one-sided hierarchy intervals (`vgen.py:432–467`). `vband` implements that tighter policy. The old live policy commonly retains `[-1,1]` until its stronger point certificate is available. `REPORT.md:1021–1079` explicitly displays and describes this difference; the aggregate band differences consequently need not be independent defects.

For example, with the incumbent successful at cost 10 and the candidate pending with elapsed lower bound 9.6, a candidate success cannot win on cost: `10-9.6 <= .05*10`. Its eventual hierarchy score can be -1 or 0, so the tighter interval `[-1,0]` is defensible; `[-1,1]` is also conservative. Exact agreement is an object-specification requirement, while containment is the validity requirement. This example is not a counterexample to either coverage theorem. The report's universal phrase “DISAGREEMENT = DEFECT” must be adjudicated at that level rather than treated as proof that one interval is invalid.

Root's subsequently proposed change is acceptable in principle: make the fully specified tighter rule the v2 live primary, independently verify containment and update/boundary semantics, align actual live and CPU pins, and preserve the conservative v1. This is **post-v1-CPU-result development and pre-live amendment**, not globally outcome-free prespecification. Replaying unchanged seed coordinates supports a paired algorithm comparison, not a fresh independent calibration sample. No alpha, margin, horizon, decision threshold, favorable-effect requirement, or seed selection need change. Detailed enclosure-boundary validation remains a prerequisite for v2 approval.

This is consistent with the old sampled-look protocol but does not implement root's corrected all-required-completion-look comparison. `vcompare` also compares enrollment prefixes plus the finalization look, rather than repairing baseline operating-characteristic measurement.

### 4. Monte Carlo wording still overstates the flag's meaning

The numerical Wilson calculation is correct (`vrun.py:104–118,655–745`), and the critical counts 64/8,000 and 147/20,000 are correct **observed-count thresholds**. They are not minimum detectable true rates or calibrated power. `PROTOCOL.md:169–173,1018–1027` still says a smaller violation “would pass unflagged” and makes an ADAPTER flag a defect verdict. As a concrete analytic counterexample, if the true event rate is .007, below .008, then at N=8,000 the probability of observing at least 64 events is **0.1571523834**. I computed that binomial tail without random draws. A flag remains a sampling alert requiring diagnosis; multiple inspected flags are not a simultaneous Monte Carlo theorem.

Likewise, `REPORT.md:25` saying the apparatus “validated itself” because its positive control fired is too strong. Preserve its actual PASS verdict, but a passing sensitivity contrast does not audit truth construction, event completeness, or the implemented primary object. The old automatic failure-consequence language remains in `vrun.py:1237–1258` and the protocol. Root's v2 interpretation should supersede it explicitly while retaining the original verdict and document history.
