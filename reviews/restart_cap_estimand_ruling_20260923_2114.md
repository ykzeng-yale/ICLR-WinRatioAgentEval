## Correction and prospective amendment

The [20:40 no-go review](prerun_bundle_go_nogo_20260923_2040.md) said that a restart-cap abort would leave “no deployment/harm decision.” That was too broad. Frozen protocol §6.4 explicitly says a **post-decision** abort truncates only the follow-up cohort: the logged decision and prefix `tau` stand, while missing follow-up arrivals are counted; §14.6 permits an aborted trial to claim a decision already logged. Conversely, the decision claim in §1.4 requires an external decision receipt, and §12.4 requires its blocking anchor/receipt before post-decision assignment.

The new three-restart cap per server per trial must therefore be phase-aware. A fourth required restart **before** a valid decision prevents a new deploy/harm decision; preserve the enrolled prefix, band endpoints, attempts, failures, unknown usage and missingness, and report the incomplete abort without treating it as a null or valid abstention. A fourth required restart **after** a valid, logged and externally receipted decision retains that earlier decision at the original `tau`; it truncates follow-up and reports every unrun arrival. It must not revise a band, score, `n`, decision time or claim from the earlier prefix. A logged decision with a pending or failed blocking receipt is retained as a provisional event, not a finalized external decision; follow the existing pause/recovery/refusal rules and do not dispatch post-switch work without the receipt. No replacement trial, extra pair, alpha transfer or margin change. The amendment should state these cases and test each one prospectively before any outcomes.

The restart cap may be a top-level infrastructure setting outside the statistical rule block **only if** it cannot change the reference monitor, alpha allocation, success margin, score, enrollment-indexed band or first crossing. It still changes operational completion and possibly observed exposure: disclose it in the protocol, config, architecture, report and immutable successor pins. A stable `rule_block_sha256` is a consequence of verified separation, not a reason to omit the cap from frozen evidence. An arm-related crash remains possible, so predecision cap-aborted trials must not be reclassified as favorable, unfavorable or null outcomes.
