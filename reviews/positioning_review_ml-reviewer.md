## 0. One-paragraph verdict

The memo is unusually honest about labels (corollary / adaptation / restatement), and the theory has been independently checked. But honesty about labels does not create a contribution, and when I trace the memo's headline empirical claims back to `results/benchmarks/decision_matrix.csv`, the two findings the abstracts lead with are not statistically resolved, and the proposed decision rule (the guarded hierarchical gate) never produces a ship/no-ship decision on real agent data that success-only inference does not already produce. As planned, this is a paper whose theorems are, by its own account, one corollary of Lindon & Malek (2022), several instances of Howard et al. / Waudby-Smith & Ramdas / Ham et al. / Mao (2018) / Even & Josse (2026), and one union-bound remark; whose "online" claims rest on synthetic streams, replay of 2024-25 benchmark logs and a USD 4 pilot; and whose decision-relevance evidence is a disagreement table in which the disagreements that matter are inside the confidence intervals. An ICLR reviewer pool for "LLM/agent evaluation" would score contribution 1-2 and rate it reject. The path to a defensible paper exists but requires changing what the paper is about (Section 6).

## 2. Fatal flaws (any one of these, unaddressed, yields reject)

**F1. The empirical centerpiece is not statistically resolved, and the abstracts lead with it.** Both "sign flip" pairs and the NB-vs-utility half of "three rules, three winners" are inside pointwise, unadjusted, task-bootstrap intervals conditional on four shared seeds (R2-E1) and, for SWE, on unmodelled repository clustering (R2-E2). The memo's own objection 6 cites Bowyer et al. (ICML 2025) on CLT failure below a few hundred datapoints; the paper is then built on 50-114 task domains and 25 exploratory contrasts. A reviewer who opens the CSV will conclude the paper cherry-picked point estimates. *Fix:* report every contrast with rank-set / simultaneous inference over the whole family; state the sign-flip result as "the two targets differ by 0.07 on the archived pool; neither is separated from zero"; remove sign-flip language from the abstract unless a powered prospective stream reproduces it.

**F2. The proposed decision rule adds nothing on the paper's own real data.** The guarded rule coincides with success-only wherever it decides (10/25) and refuses wherever the hierarchy alone would decide (9/25). So the "decision layer with guarantees" is, empirically, a non-inferiority test on success with extra refusals. The one place the hierarchy could add value, resolving a comparison when success ties (airline GPT-4.1 vs o4-mini, NB -0.16 [-0.31, -0.01], success diff -0.03 [-0.13, 0.07]), is exactly where the 3-pp guardrail blocks it. Reviewer question: "What decision does your method make that a success-NI test plus a paired cost test does not?" The memo has no answer, and the protocol's own mandatory comparator 6 ("success noninferiority plus efficiency improvement decision rule", i.e. the Spotify rule) is **absent from `decision_disagreement.py`**. *Fix:* implement the Spotify-style rule (success NI at margin AND cost/latency superiority, both sequential) as the primary comparator; show, in replay at realistic online n and in the prospective stream, whether the guarded hierarchical rule decides earlier, more often, or differently, at matched error control. If it does not, the paper is a reporting protocol, not a decision method, and must be sold as such.

**F3. The theorem list is, by the memo's own labels, one corollary, several adaptations and one remark, with the corollary dominated by an existing method.** Theorem 1 (multinomial CS for every win statistic) is a direct application of Lindon & Malek Thm 2.4 plus convex optimization; its own comparison shows it is 15-55% wider and 40% less powerful than the betting CS the paper also uses. Theorem 2 is Ham et al. / Howard et al. instantiated. Theorem 3(a) is the fixed-sample IUT logic Schultzberg et al. already use, stated sequentially; 3(b) is a union bound; 3(c) is Li-Fan-Yang plus a union bound. Propositions 1-2 are Mao (2018) / Even & Josse (2026) / Buyse (2010) / Fu (2026) restated for agents. At ICLR, "contribution" scores of 1-2 follow. *Fix:* stop presenting these as theorems in the main text. Either (i) find one genuinely new technical result (candidates in Section 6), or (ii) reframe as an evaluation-methodology paper whose contribution is a named diagnostic plus a large, surprising empirical finding, in which case the theorems are appendix hygiene.

## 3. Major concerns (each would cost a point or two; together they compound F1-F3)

**M1. The "online" half has no online data.** All three abstracts say "under continuous online monitoring" / "pair-randomized A/B tests". The evidence is (a) synthetic streams, (b) replay of historical benchmark logs from 2024-25 (tau2, SWE-bench Lite, HAL), and (c) a 12-task, USD 4 Haiku pilot. The memo itself concedes a powered stream needs USD 30-60 and keys by 09-19. *Fix:* either fund E7 now (three cheap agents, airline 50 tasks x 4 trials, independently generated task-specific seeds, randomized scheduling, prespecified rules) or rewrite abstract and title to say "offline benchmark comparison and replay", and move the online design to a "protocol" section explicitly labelled unexecuted.

**M2. The cross-arrival estimand is ill-posed for the cost tier, so the "Hand's paradox on real data" story is suspect even if it were significant.** In `run_replay.py` the cross-arrival design draws independent tasks for A and B. The hierarchical kernel then compares the cost of task X under A with the cost of task Y under B. That contrast measures task heterogeneity, not system difference; no practitioner wants it. The retail offset between tau_* and tau_pop is more plausibly a cost-across-tasks artifact than a Hand's-paradox mechanism. *Fix:* stratify the online design by an outcome-blind task family (the theory already allows fixed strata) or restrict the cross-arrival hierarchy to tiers that are meaningful across requests; state explicitly that NB_1 = p_A - p_B for binary success under independence, so the cross-arrival success tier is just the success difference.

**M3. The hierarchy with a 5% cost tolerance is a poor preference rule, and the guardrail is a patch for its own defect.** With 60% of retail pairs jointly successful and a 5% relative cost tolerance, cost decides most comparisons; the "reversal" (NB 0.53 with success -0.07) is the rule saying a 6x cheaper system wins even though it fails 7 pp more often. That is not a discovery about agent evaluation; it is the definition of the rule. A reviewer will ask why the tolerance is not 50%, why success non-inferiority is not tier 0, or why not a thresholded lexicographic order (Tercan & Prabhu, ECAI 2024, in `lit_agenteval.md`). *Fix:* justify the tolerance from measurement noise (cost variance across seeds within task, which the four trials let you estimate) or from an elicited preference; report the tolerance at which each reversal disappears; include "success NI as tier 0" as a hierarchy in the prespecified family so Theorem 3(c) has teeth.

**M4. Stale, small, and defect-laden datasets relative to the ICLR exemplar bar.** tau2 runs are 2025 snapshots on a harness whose maintainers have since corrected tasks (tau3 fixes; SABER), with 114 vs 115 retail tasks unexplained and four shared seeds per domain. SWE-bench Lite runs are April 2024 GPT-4 and Claude 3 Opus, with 114 Django + 77 SymPy tasks and no cluster-valid interval. HAL airline is 50 tasks, one trial, NB intervals about +/-0.2 wide. The accepted analogues (HAL: 21,730 rollouts; LLMs Get Lost: 200K conversations; Chatbot Arena: 240K votes) set a different scale. *Fix:* at minimum add AgentBoard logs (`empirical_feasibility.md` identifies them) or fresh runs on current models; report a repository-cluster interval for SWE; drop HAL inferential language or pool more HAL benchmarks.

**M5. Multiplicity and exploratory status of the headline contrasts.** The protocol designates o4-mini vs GPT-4.1 as the main contrast; the reversals are Claude contrasts, exploratory by design (R2-E5). The tolerance grid (0/5/10/20%) and two priority orders multiply the family further, and R2-E6 already shows interval exclusion is not robust to tolerance (airline at 0%, telecom at 20% include zero). *Fix:* one confirmatory contrast per dataset with the family fixed in advance; everything else in a clearly labelled exploratory table with simultaneous bands.

**M6. Objection 7 (efficiency) is currently answered with promises.** E8 (all-pairs vs disjoint-pair variance ratio) is "to do"; the GST (Zhang-Wu / Bergemann-Hanson), Cai-Hu-Li AsympCS and Kotawala e-process comparators are queued as GitHub issue 3 with no owner. The only competitive comparator run is the betting CS, which the paper itself uses. *Fix:* run at least Bergemann-Hanson GST with HSD gamma = -3 at K = 5 and Kotawala's paired-Bernoulli e-process on the success tier for one scenario and one replay stream; report width and stopping time at matched alpha; compute the variance ratio on the empirical cell frequencies.

**M7. Theorem 1's promised simultaneity is untested at the cell count it needs.** The 3-cell width price is measured; the 3^T-cell (27 for T = 3) version needed for "every tier contribution, every component-marginal NB and every hierarchy from one set" is not implemented, and with a uniform Dirichlet prior over 27 cells the width at 100 pairs will be far worse. *Fix:* implement the 27-cell version with the informative prior alpha_0 = k theta_0, report widths, or drop the "every hierarchy" claim.

**M8. Measurement validity.** The abstracts speak of "policy violations" and "compliance", but no dataset has a validated policy label (the memo admits this); the compliance tier exists only in simulation. Kirgis et al. (2026) show pass/fail hides shortcuts on tau-bench Airline; the label-contamination sensitivity (0/1/5%) has not been executed. *Fix:* remove policy/compliance vocabulary from abstract and thesis unless a judged tier is actually measured; run the contamination sensitivity.

**M9. Presentation load in 9 pages.** Two propositions, three theorems, ten appendix items, nine experiments, a protocol box, five figures and two tables, in clinical vocabulary. The project's own rubric notes the closest accepted analogues scored presentation 1-2 (UNVERIFIED by me). *Fix:* one named object (e.g. "priority inversion" with the tier-decomposition identity), one figure that shows it on real data by page 2, one theorem in the main text, everything else in the appendix.

**M10. Replay "paired 96% vs cross-arrival 33% deployment" is not evidence for anything new.** Pairing reduces variance; Miller (2024) and Chapelle et al. (2012) interleaving already say so, and the two designs estimate different targets, so their deployment rates are not comparable operating characteristics. *Fix:* present as an illustration of the estimand difference, not as a design result.

**M11. Theorem 3(b)'s counterexample answers a claim no one makes.** The fixed-sample IUT guarantee (Schultzberg et al.) is for a fixed null; nobody claims per-gate alpha controls "ever deploy while some *current* running-average gate is false" under arbitrary drift. Defining the error that way and then observing you need Bonferroni is a definitional remark. *Fix:* keep as a remark with the alternating-block example in the appendix; do not count it as a contribution.

## 5. Would the framework change a real decision?

On the paper's own 25 real contrasts: no. The guarded rule reproduces success-only decisions and blocks every hierarchical-only decision. In simulation it does what any NI-gate does. The strongest honest sentence available is: "a success-first lexicographic preference with a small cost tolerance will, on released tau2 logs, prefer a 6x cheaper model that fails 7 pp more often; any deployment rule must therefore couple the preference with a success guardrail, at which point, at benchmark sample sizes, the guardrail decides." That is a cautionary reporting recommendation, useful for a workshop or a TMLR-style methodology note, not an ICLR contribution as currently framed.

## 6. Constructive path (ranked by expected score gain per day, deadline 2026-09-25)

1. **Implement the Spotify rule as the primary comparator and report where the guarded hierarchical rule differs** (0.5 day). If it never differs on real data, pivot the paper (item 6). If it differs in replay at online n, that is the paper's figure 1.
2. **Kill the unresolved headlines.** Remove "differ in sign" and "three rules, three winners" from the abstract; replace with the one resolved inversion (retail o4-mini vs Claude 3.7: NB 0.53 [0.42, 0.63], success -0.07 [-0.14, -0.00]) labelled exploratory, and with rank-set inference for rankings (1 day).
3. **Fund and run the prospective stream (E7)** with independent seeds and prespecified rules, or rewrite title/abstract as offline + replay (decision by 09-19).
4. **Run the missing sequential comparators** (Bergemann-Hanson GST, Kotawala e-process on success, Cai-Hu-Li AsympCS one-sample adaptation) and the disjoint-vs-all-pairs variance ratio on empirical cells (1-2 days). Without them objection 7 is unanswered.
5. **Demote Theorem 1 to an appendix remark** unless the 27-cell informative-prior version closes most of the width gap; keep the betting CS as the method. Make the shared-seed lemma and the delayed-outcome envelope the main-text technical content, since those are the only items reviewers cannot find elsewhere.
6. **Reframe as an evaluation-methodology paper** with one named diagnostic ("priority inversion" prevalence via the tier-decomposition identity, with prevalence and consequence tables across tau2 x SWE x HAL x AgentBoard), one protocol box, and a released package. In that framing the theorems are hygiene and the reviewers' question becomes "did we learn something about agent evaluation?", which the inversion prevalence can answer if it is shown with simultaneous inference on more than three domains.
7. **Fix M2 and M3 in the design**: stratified cross-arrival pairs; tolerance justified from within-task seed variance; "success NI as tier 0" in the prespecified hierarchy family.
8. **Cut policy/compliance vocabulary** from abstract and thesis until a measured tier exists.
9. Keep all existing honesty (labels, deviation ledger, pointwise caveats); it is the paper's best feature and is what will get a rebuttal read.

If the authors cannot do items 1-4 by the deadline, the more credible plan is a workshop or TMLR submission of the reporting protocol plus the shared-seed and delayed-outcome results, and an ICLR submission next cycle with a powered prospective stream and current-model data.
