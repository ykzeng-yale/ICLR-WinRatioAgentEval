## What the existing unfavorable results teach us

The new deterministic analysis is `experiments/build_scientific_diagnostics.py`, with source hashes and output in `results/scientific_diagnostics/diagnosis.json`. It reads saved metrics and nonexecuted source text; it makes no model calls, reruns no experiment and attaches no new inferential interval. Its numerical results are post-hoc descriptions of the existing schedule, pending manuscript integration.

**Coding workflow hypothesis.** On the 591 same-task descriptions, both workflows passed the hidden verifier on 433 tasks. The repair workflow B succeeded where A failed on 40 tasks, and failed where A succeeded on another 40; 393 were joint successes and 118 joint failures. B used 4.456 times the workflow time, 4.461 times the completion tokens, and 6.329 times the total tokens. The same-task hierarchy has success contribution zero and resource contribution `1 - 389 = -388`, giving `-388/591 = -0.6565`. This observed ranking is explained by the resource penalty after joint success, rather than a reversed score sign or an unexplained statistical anomaly. It contradicts a claim of an aggregate success improvement in these records. It does not establish that repair caused each rescue/regression: initial samples, periods and histories differ, and intermediate counterfactual solutions were not randomized for that question.

**Intermediate metric diagnosis.** The archived collector sets `self_test_passed` from the last executed generated-test program and then sends the final candidate to the hidden verifier (`agent.py.txt:231–241`). All 591 B records have a Boolean flag and no recorded outer episode error. Their contingency table is:

| Final generated self-tests | Hidden verifier success | Hidden verifier failure |
|---|---:|---:|
| Pass | 267 | 51 |
| Fail | 166 | 107 |

There are 217 disagreements. A passing self-test is not equivalent to final benchmark success; a failing self-test is not equivalent to its failure. Possible mechanisms include inadequate or incorrect generated assertions, execution-environment differences and incomplete benchmark checks. The retained metric projection cannot adjudicate these mechanisms; it is not evidence that 217 labels are objectively wrong. Root therefore keeps the external hidden-verifier endpoint primary and treats self-tests as a process diagnostic. Counts by repair depth are also descriptive, because depth is selected by prior failures; their success rates are not treatment effects of another repair step. A future trace audit or intervention on test generation would need a separate question and retained intermediate artifacts. No new model study or benchmark replacement is authorized by this observation.

**Hierarchy versus population success.** For this binary-success hierarchy with joint failures tied, the exact per-pair identity is `H = D + J*C`: `D` is candidate-minus-incumbent success, `J` indicates joint success, and `C` is the resource-tier sign including ties. Thus `E[H] = E[D] + E[J*C]`. Prioritizing success within a pair does not guarantee population success preservation: a candidate failing on 10% of pairs but cheaper on the remaining joint-success 90% can have mean `D=-.1` and mean `H=.8`. This elementary constructed law explains why the separate success guardrail is necessary. Root retains it and does not choose a weaker margin to obtain a win. The hierarchy is a declared preference, not a universally optimal utility or a monetary savings estimator. Joint-failure costs still belong in all-attempt resource summaries even when the hierarchy ties those pairs.

**Pairing and sparse resource comparisons.** In the coding first pass, the exact decomposition is `15 - 154 = -139` across 295 cross-arrival pairs, whereas the complementary same-task description gives `0 - 388 = -388` across 591 tasks. These are different comparisons, so different values do not establish an implementation error. The first-pass analysis cannot silently become an individual same-task causal effect. In the airline replay, all 19 non-ties among 49 pairs were decided by success; the resource contribution is zero. That experiment provides no observed decisive resource-tier contrast, and incomplete failed-attempt usage additionally prevents an operational cost-efficiency claim. Low benchmark success can make an ostensibly multi-metric comparison empirically almost binary. Root must report tier reach and contribution, rather than interpreting an unvisited tier as evidence of equal resources.

## Theory validity and practical adequacy are different tests

The inspected normal-mixture argument uses bounded centered increments, a predictable variance bound, Gaussian mixing and Ville's inequality. The asynchronous theorem transfers coverage by simultaneous containment of latent final scores; it does not require a new calendar-time martingale. None of the observed design mismatches supplies a counterexample to those inspected steps under their stated assumptions. This is a bounded reassessment, not a fresh certification of every theorem in the repository. A changed target, invalid enclosure, unrecorded assignment dependence or false runtime assumption could invalidate an application while the conditional theorem remains correct.

Practical adequacy is a real root-design limitation. The `.03` margin is an illustrative protocol choice, not an independently elicited application tolerance. For the prescribed `alpha_gate=.00625`, `rho=100`, and complete scores, `r(n)=sqrt((n+100)*log((n+100)/(100*.00625^2)))/n`. At the pre-freeze upper ceiling 565, `r=.1584036326`; certifying `L_success>-.03` requires an observed difference above `.1284036326`, at least 73 net extra successes across 565 assigned pairs. Exactly zero observed difference first satisfies this inequality at 17,097 pairs. These are conditional algebraic facts, **not live power probabilities, guarantees of abstention or a proposed larger collection**. The actual horizon may be smaller; partial information can make certification harder. Positive outcomes can still cross at smaller horizons. Root should have foregrounded this mismatch between a narrow tolerance and the resource-feasible sample size.

The approved serialization of pair enrollment also leaves at most one unfinished pair. For raw, unclipped bands the exact width is `2*r(n) + sum_i(u_i-l_i)/n`. One unresolved bounded score contributes at most `2/n` to this width. For the binary success difference after one arm's success is known, its enclosure width is at most 1, giving at most `1/n`; that tighter statement is not automatic for the hierarchy's resource comparison. The live design therefore offers limited leverage for demonstrating large gains from many out-of-order pending pairs. Root retains the modest live claim and assigns substantial-delay comparisons to the specified CPU laws. A broader scheduler or sharper variance-adaptive rule is a future design/theory problem, not a silent repair to this trial.

## How root will judge a concerning result

| Observed concern | First discriminating check | Scientific disposition |
|---|---|---|
| Two implementations disagree | Same estimand, enclosure policy, pin, event time and floating boundary? Independently check containment. | Fix the specification or implementation identified by the witness; no blanket 'theory failed'. |
| Coverage alert | Reconstruct truth, latent scores, all required looks, repetitions and nominal event; retain MC uncertainty. | Distinguish sampler/harness bug, assumption failure, genuine mathematical counterexample and random alert. |
| Correct rule rarely deploys | Complete-score gate slack, radius, unresolved width, true/observed component effects. | Report underpowered/low-signal/guardrail-limited feasibility as appropriate; do not retune toward significance. |

| Resource advantage appears | Full attempts, endpoint definition, servicing/period conditions and units. | Restrict interpretation to measured quantities; tokenizer counts are not universal compute or dollars. |
