## 1. Thesis and contributions

### Thesis

Agent teams already collect prioritized, heterogeneous, tie-prone outcomes for every run (verifier success, policy violations, tool calls, latency, cost), already compare candidates pairwise on the same inputs (LangSmith and Braintrust record per-input wins/ties/losses; Statsig AI Evals shadow-runs candidates on live traffic), and already monitor rollouts continuously (LaunchDarkly checks guarded rollouts "multiple times per minute"; Eppo and GrowthBook ship confidence sequences); what they lack is a decision rule whose error guarantees survive both the prioritization and the monitoring. Clinical trials solved the prioritization half with generalized pairwise comparisons and win statistics, and the sequential-inference community solved the monitoring half with confidence sequences and e-processes; joining them for agent comparison exposes two facts neither community has had to state: (i) offline task-matched benchmarks, pair-randomized single-exposure A/B tests and shadow execution identify three different win estimands, and on released tau2-bench trajectories the offline and cross-arrival targets already differ in sign for one domain; (ii) because lower tiers are consulted only when higher tiers tie, a hierarchical winner can be strictly worse on task success — on tau2-bench retail, o4-mini is preferred to Claude 3.7 Sonnet with net benefit 0.53 while its success rate is significantly lower — so a valid deployment gate must couple the pairwise preference with component-level non-inferiority guardrails whose sequential error control differs between stationary and drifting traffic. We give the estimand map, finite-sample confidence sequences (one exact set that simultaneously covers every win statistic, tier contribution and prespecified hierarchy; one variance-adaptive scalar sequence that is the operational gate), a guarded sequential decision rule with stated error control, and evidence on tau2-bench, SWE-bench Lite and HAL trajectories that success-only rankings, Pareto membership, linear cost-utility rules and the guarded hierarchical decision name different winners once task-clustered uncertainty is respected.

## 3. Theorem list

Target: two propositions and three theorems in the main text; the rest in the appendix. Labels: new / adaptation / restatement (corollary). Existing `theory.tex` results are mapped in brackets.

### Main text

**Proposition 1 (What each design identifies)** [theory.tex: identification example; pair-randomization theorem; offline theorem; shared-seed lemma]. Informal: with an antisymmetric hierarchical rule h on outcomes observed up to a prespecified horizon c, (a) offline replicate sets with prespecified task weights give an unbiased estimate of sum_t w_t tau_*(t), with off-diagonal seed pairs targeting the independent-copy quantity and all R^2 pairs targeting (1-1/R) theta_ind + (1/R) theta_coupled; (b) disjoint AB/BA-randomized arrival pairs give unbiased draws from the cross-arrival cell vector theta_pop (NB = Mao's tau_h); (c) no single-exposure design identifies the same-request preference (cyclic couplings with equal marginals, same-request NB +1/3 and -1/3, cross-arrival NB 0); (d) shadow execution identifies it directly. Assumptions: no carryover within a pair; fixed horizon; randomization independent of potential outcomes given the stratum; prespecified w and h; independent complete task records (R2.1). Proof: linearity and exchangeability; explicit construction. Relies on Mao 2018, Even & Josse 2026, Lee et al. 2026, Oakes 2016 / Mao 2024. Novelty: adaptation; (c) restatement.

**Proposition 2 (Tier decomposition and primary-outcome inversion)** [theory.tex: compensation counterexample]. NB = sum_k P(reach k) NB_k; with binary success at tier 1 and independent copies NB_1 = p_A - p_B, so NB - (p_A - p_B) = P(primary tie) x NB(lower | tie); hence NB -> 1 with p_A - p_B = -epsilon (ours) and p_A > p_B componentwise with NB < 0 (Fu 2026) are both possible. Proof: total expectation over the decisive tier. Relies on Buyse 2010, McCoy et al. 2026, Fu 2026, Verbeeck 2019. Novelty: restatement.

**Theorem 1 (One confidence sequence for every win statistic; i.i.d. route)** [not yet in theory.tex; proof about one page]. For i.i.d. comparison vectors X_i ~ Multinomial(1, theta) over the 3^T (or 2T+1) cells, C_n = {theta : O_n(theta) < 1/alpha} with the Dirichlet-mixture likelihood ratio O_n satisfies P(theta in C_n for all n) >= 1 - alpha; simultaneously for all n and all f in F = {NB, WR, WO, DOOR, tier contributions, component-marginal pairwise NBs, NB_r for r in a prespecified finite family}, f(theta) in [inf_{C_n} f, sup_{C_n} f]; linear functionals are convex programs, ratio functionals reduce to linear feasibility by level sets; the WR upper bound is +infinity when C_n meets {p_L = 0}. Assumptions: i.i.d. arrivals within stratum (no drift), orientation randomization (Lemma A1), partition and family fixed in advance. Proof: Lindon-Malek Theorems 2.1-2.4; convexity; quasi-linearity. Novelty: corollary; the functional/simultaneity remarks and the 3^T design are ours. Report width and power price from `cs_width.csv` and `online_methods_results.csv`, and rerun with an informative prior.

**Theorem 2 (Bounded-score confidence sequence under adaptive orientation and drift)** [theory.tex: normal-mixture CS; betting validity]. HT-weighted score bounded by 1/(2 epsilon) with conditional mean the pair's conditional NB; normal-mixture or betting CS with predictable ranges covers the running average of conditional NBs uniformly in n; under i.i.d. it covers NB; under drift a running conditional average only. Assumptions: predictable orientation in [epsilon, 1-epsilon], predictable bounds, fixed horizon. Proof: complete in theory.tex; present as an instance of Ham et al.; cite Howard and Waudby-Smith & Ramdas. Novelty: adaptation. Two sentences in the main text.

**Theorem 3 (Guarded sequential decision rule)** [theory.tex: stationary conjunction; drifting conjunction; plus one new part]. Deploy at n iff the NB lower bound exceeds its margin and every guardrail lower bound exceeds -delta_j. (a) Stationary: each sequence at level alpha gives P(ever deploy while some gate is false) <= alpha, no split; under Theorem 1 all intervals are also simultaneously valid. (b) Drifting: a sequence of distributions exists under which per-gate alpha deploys while some gate is currently false with probability -> 1; budgets summing to alpha restore control. (c) Worst case over a family: min_r lower-bound_n(NB_r) is a valid anytime lower bound on min_r NB_r. Assumptions: gates, margins and family fixed in advance; deployment is a stopping decision; the reported rule is current-look conjunction (R2.2). Proof: union null and single-gate error (a); alternating-block construction (b); minimum of simultaneous bounds (c). Relies on Berger-type IUT as used by Schultzberg et al.; Karampatziakis et al. 2021; Li-Fan-Yang 2026; PEAK as the alternative. Novelty: (a) adaptation, (b) new (small), (c) adaptation.

## 6. Three alternative abstracts and title candidates

Consistent with what Sections 3-4 can deliver without a powered prospective experiment. Bracketed [x] values are computable from existing tables but not yet computed as a single count; all other numbers are from `results/`.

### Abstract A (decision-layer framing; ~235 words)

Teams comparing two versions of an AI agent already record prioritized, heterogeneous outcomes per run — task success, policy violations, tool calls, latency, cost — already compare candidates pairwise on the same inputs, and already monitor rollouts continuously. What they lack is a decision rule whose error guarantees survive both the prioritization and the monitoring. We adapt hierarchical win statistics from clinical trials (net benefit, win ratio, win odds) to agent comparison and join them with anytime-valid inference. First, we show which win estimand each evaluation design identifies: offline task-matched replicates identify a same-task preference, pair-randomized single-exposure A/B tests identify only a cross-request contrast, and shadow execution identifies the same-request preference at double cost; on released tau2-bench trajectories the first two already differ in sign for one domain. Second, because each randomized pair's comparison is one multinomial draw, a single time-uniform confidence set yields simultaneously valid confidence sequences for every win statistic, tier contribution and prespecified alternative hierarchy under optional stopping, while a variance-adaptive bounded-score sequence serves as the operational gate under adaptive assignment and drift. Third, since lower tiers are consulted only when higher tiers tie, a hierarchical winner can be strictly worse on task success — on tau2-bench retail one system is preferred with net benefit 0.53 despite a significantly lower success rate — so we give a guarded sequential rule with intersection-union error control and show why drifting traffic needs a different error budget. In simulation the guarded rule falsely deployed in 0.45% of identical-agent runs versus 31% for repeatedly inspected intervals; on tau2-bench, SWE-bench Lite and HAL trajectories, success-only, Pareto, linear-utility and guarded hierarchical rules name different winners in [x] of [y] comparisons.

### Abstract B (estimand and guarantee first; ~225 words)

Comparing AI agent systems requires trading off task success against policy compliance, cost and latency, across tasks whose outcomes are tie-prone and observed under budget caps, increasingly under continuous online monitoring. We formalize this comparison as a prioritized pairwise (win/tie/loss) contrast with prespecified tolerances and ask three questions. What does each observation design identify? Offline replicate benchmarks estimate a task-conditional independent-copy win probability, randomized single-exposure online pairs estimate the population win contrast of Mao (2018), and neither identifies the same-request preference, which only shadow execution recovers; reused simulator seeds shift the offline target by a quantified amount. How can it be monitored continuously? Treating each randomized pair's comparison vector as a multinomial draw, the Dirichlet-mixture confidence sequence of Lindon and Malek (2022) yields, from one convex set, simultaneous anytime-valid intervals for net benefit, win ratio, win odds, tier contributions and a whole family of hierarchies; a design-based bounded-score confidence sequence handles adaptive orientation and drift, and we quantify the width each route pays. What does a positive result license? Because lower tiers are consulted only among primary ties, hierarchical preference can invert the primary effect; we give a guarded sequential deployment rule with intersection-union control whose error budget must be split under drift. On tau2-bench, SWE-bench Lite and HAL trajectories the preference and the success difference favor opposite systems in two of three tau2 domains, and the paired and cross-arrival targets differ in sign in one.

### Abstract C (empirical finding first; ~215 words)

Agent leaderboards rank systems by task success, cost-aware evaluations draw Pareto frontiers, and production teams gate rollouts on per-metric guardrails; none yields a single decision with an error guarantee when one system succeeds slightly less often but costs a third as much, or when success ties on most tasks. We re-analyze released tau2-bench (three models, three domains, four seed-matched trials), SWE-bench Lite (two agents, 300 tasks) and HAL airline (two scaffolds, three models) trajectories with a prespecified hierarchy — success, then cost, then tool calls, with practical-equivalence tolerances — and find that in tau2-bench retail the success rate, the hierarchical net benefit and a linear cost-utility score each name a different winner; that in retail and telecom the preferred system has a significantly lower success rate (net benefit 0.53 and 0.26 against success differences of -0.07); and that swapping two lower tiers flips a sign. To make such decisions safely online, we show which win estimand pair-randomized A/B data identify (and that it differs in sign from the offline target in one domain), derive from the multinomial confidence sequence of Lindon and Malek simultaneous anytime-valid intervals for every win statistic and tier contribution of a paired stream, and give a guarded sequential deployment rule with intersection-union error control that must split its budget under drift. In randomized stream replay the guarded rule resolves a pooled comparison after a median of 805 paired runs with no harmful deployments.
