## 1. Thesis and contributions

### Thesis

Agent teams record prioritized, heterogeneous, tie-prone outcomes per run (verifier success, cost, tool calls, latency), compare candidates pairwise on the same inputs, and gate rollouts on per-metric guardrails; what none of these practices states is what a prioritized pairwise preference *estimates* under a given evaluation design, or when acting on it is safe. Adapting hierarchical win statistics from clinical trials makes both questions precise, and answering them on released agent trajectories yields three findings. (i) Because lower tiers are consulted only when higher tiers tie, a hierarchical preference can invert the primary effect: on tau2-bench, SWE-bench Lite and HAL trajectories 9 of 25 contrasts show a **priority inversion** (hierarchy prefers the system with lower success), 4 with net-benefit intervals excluding zero, the largest being retail o4-mini vs Claude 3.7 Sonnet (NB 0.53 [0.42, 0.63] against a success difference of -0.07 [-0.14, -0.00]); inversions are driven by cost ratios near 6x and vanish at tolerances we report, while marginal hierarchical decisions flip under 1% label noise in 27.5% of contaminated replicates. (ii) Offline task-matched replicates, pair-randomized single-exposure A/B streams and shadow execution identify three different pairwise estimands; on the same tau2 pool the offline and cross-arrival targets differ by 0.05 on average and the offline target resolves the airline contrast (NB -0.16 [-0.31, -0.01]) where the cross-arrival target does not (-0.056 [-0.139, 0.037]); resource tiers are meaningful online only within prespecified request strata. (iii) A guarded sequential rule built from bounded-score confidence sequences never deployed a success-regressing system in 2,000 simulated streams (where the unguarded win statistic deployed in all), resolved a pooled contrast after a median of 838 paired runs in randomized stream replay, and, at benchmark sample sizes, decides exactly when the incumbent success-guardrail conjunction does: the hierarchy is a reporting and preference device, and the guardrail is what makes it safe. We state the guarantees (stationary intersection-union control at alpha; m alpha per-gate under drift, alpha with a split), measure the price of disjoint-pair anytime validity against all-pairs group-sequential and asymptotic confidence-sequence methods, and give a partial-score envelope that certifies pair outcomes from partially resolved tiers before slow episodes complete, halving decision time in simulation.

## 3. Theorem list (v2)

Main text: four propositions, two lines each, proofs in the appendix. Labels: new / adaptation / restatement.

**Proposition 1 (What each design identifies).** [theory.tex: identification example; pair-randomization theorem; offline theorem; shared-seed lemma.] (a) Offline replicate sets with prespecified task weights estimate sum_t w_t tau_*(t) without bias; off-diagonal seed pairs target the independent-copy quantity, all R^2 pairs target (1-1/R) theta_ind + (1/R) theta_coupled with bias <= 2/R. (b) Disjoint AB/BA-randomized arrivals within a prespecified stratum give unbiased draws from the stratum cross-arrival cell vector; for binary success its tier-1 contribution is p_A - p_B; resource tiers compare requests unless stratified. (c) No single-exposure design identifies the same-request preference (cyclic couplings with equal marginals; same-request NB +1/3 and -1/3; cross-arrival NB 0). (d) Shadow execution identifies the same-request preference only for outcomes computable without a live user. Assumptions: no carryover within a pair; fixed horizon; randomization independent of potential outcomes given the stratum; prespecified weights, hierarchy and strata; independent complete task records (R2.1). Novelty: (a) adaptation with a new small lemma; (b) adaptation; (c) restatement (Mao 2018; Even & Josse 2026); (d) scope condition, new as a statement.

**Proposition 2 (Tier decomposition and priority inversion).** [theory.tex: compensation counterexample.] NB = sum_k P(reach k) NB_k; with binary success first and independent copies, NB - (p_A - p_B) = P(primary tie) x NB(lower | tie). Both NB -> 1 with p_A - p_B = -epsilon and p_A > p_B componentwise with NB < 0 (Fu 2026) are possible. Novelty: restatement (Buyse 2010; McCoy et al. 2026; Fu 2026; Verbeeck 2019); the name and the measurement are ours.

**Proposition 3 (Guarantees of the guarded rule).** [theory.tex: normal-mixture CS; betting validity; stationary conjunction; drifting conjunction.] (a) The HT-weighted bounded score has conditional mean equal to the pair's conditional NB; a normal-mixture or betting CS with predictable ranges covers the running average of conditional NBs uniformly in n (instance of Ham et al.; Howard et al.; Waudby-Smith & Ramdas). (b) Stationary: with m gates each at level alpha, P(ever deploy while some gate is false) <= alpha (Berger-type IUT; no split). (c) Drifting: with per-gate alpha, P(ever deploy while some current running-average gate is false) <= m alpha, and alternating-block constructions exceed alpha; budgets summing to alpha restore alpha; the stationary proof does not transfer because the violating index may change with n; the betting variant has no running-average guarantee under drift. (d) min_r L_n(NB_r) over a prespecified finite family is an anytime lower bound on min_r NB_r (sequential Li-Fan-Yang). Assumptions: predictable orientation in [epsilon, 1-epsilon], predictable bounds, fixed horizon; gates, margins and family fixed in advance; current-look conjunction (R2.2). Novelty: (a),(b),(d) adaptation; (c) remark-level (union bound), stated correctly.

**Proposition 4 (Partial-score envelope for delayed outcomes).** [asynchronous.tex: `thm:async_cs`, `thm:async_betting`, `cor:async_envelope`.] Under deterministic enclosures for each unresolved score, (a) the full-score CS transfers pathwise to calendar time with width equal to the full-score width plus the mean unresolved width; (b) a positive-stake product at lower bounds is dominated by the full-score product, so a threshold crossing implies a crossing of the latent enrollment-order process (threshold error control at alpha; not a calendar-time e-process); (c) the prefix envelope is never later than the completed-prefix rule. Scope: verifier success, metered cost and latency admit enclosures; judged or human outcomes do not, and the envelope then equals the completed-prefix rule. Novelty: adaptation (Henzi & Ziegel 2022 + correction; Choe & Ramdas 2024); the hierarchical enclosure construction is ours.

## 6. Three alternative abstracts and title candidates

All numbers from the shipped CSVs; bracketed [x] are to be filled from E2a/E8 once run.

### Abstract A (diagnostic first; recommended; 248 words)

Teams comparing two AI agent versions record several outcomes per run (success, cost, tool calls, latency) and must decide which to ship. Leaderboards rank by success, cost-aware evaluations draw Pareto frontiers, and experimentation platforms gate rollouts on per-metric guardrails; none says what a prioritized pairwise preference estimates or when acting on it is safe. We adapt clinical-trial hierarchical win statistics (net benefit, win ratio) to agent comparison as an evaluation protocol. First, we map which estimand each design identifies: offline task-matched replicates identify a same-task preference (with quantified shared-seed bias), pair-randomized single-exposure A/B streams identify only a stratified cross-request contrast, and shadow execution identifies the same-request preference only for outcomes computable without a user. Second, we name and measure priority inversion: lower tiers are consulted only when higher tiers tie, so a hierarchical winner can have significantly lower success. On released tau2-bench, SWE-bench Lite and HAL trajectories, 9 of 25 contrasts invert; in tau2-bench retail one system is preferred with net benefit 0.53 (95% interval 0.42–0.63) despite 7 points lower success, and marginal decisions flip under 1% label noise. Third, we give a guarded sequential decision rule based on confidence sequences, state its error control under stationary and drifting traffic, and measure its price against all-pairs group-sequential and per-metric conjunction rules. It never deployed a success-regressing system in simulation and resolved a pooled contrast after a median of 838 paired runs in randomized stream replay. A partial-score envelope certifies pair outcomes before slow episodes complete, halving decision time.

### Abstract B (estimand first; ~230 words)

Comparing AI agent systems means trading task success against cost, tool use and latency across tie-prone tasks, increasingly while a rollout is monitored. We formalize the comparison as a prioritized pairwise (win/tie/loss) contrast with prespecified tolerances and ask what it estimates, when it misleads, and how to act on it. Which design identifies which estimand: offline replicate benchmarks estimate a task-conditional independent-copy preference (reused simulator seeds shift it by a quantified amount), randomized single-exposure online pairs estimate a cross-request contrast that is meaningful for resource tiers only within prespecified strata, and only shadow execution recovers the same-request preference, for outcomes computable without a user. When it misleads: lower tiers are consulted only among primary ties, so the preference can invert the primary effect; on tau2-bench, SWE-bench Lite and HAL trajectories 9 of 25 contrasts show such priority inversions, the largest with net benefit 0.53 [0.42, 0.63] against a success difference of −0.07 [−0.14, −0.00], and decisions near the boundary flip under 1% label noise. How to act: a guarded sequential rule couples the preference with component non-inferiority guardrails using bounded-score confidence sequences; we state its intersection-union control under stationary traffic and the m-fold budget under drift, benchmark it against per-metric conjunctions and all-pairs group-sequential methods, and extend it with a partial-score envelope that uses partially resolved tiers of slow episodes. In simulation and randomized stream replay the guarded rule never deployed a success-regressing system and resolved a pooled contrast after a median of 838 paired runs.

### Abstract C (protocol first; ~225 words)

Production teams already compare agent candidates pairwise on the same inputs and gate rollouts on guardrails, but the pairwise preference they compute has no stated estimand and its safety is unexamined. We give an evaluation protocol for prioritized pairwise comparison of AI agents based on hierarchical win statistics and confidence sequences, and test it on released tau2-bench, SWE-bench Lite and HAL trajectories and in randomized stream replay. The protocol fixes a hierarchy and tolerances before outcomes are read, states which of three estimands the evaluation design identifies (same-task, stratified cross-request, same-request), reports the tier decomposition that exposes priority inversions (a hierarchical winner with lower success: 9 of 25 real contrasts, one with net benefit 0.53 [0.42, 0.63] against a success difference of −0.07), and deploys only when a net-benefit confidence sequence and every component guardrail agree, with error control stated for stationary and drifting traffic. We measure what the protocol costs: at benchmark sample sizes the guardrail decides and the hierarchy is a reporting device; in simulation the guarded rule never deployed a success-regressing system where the unguarded statistic always did; anytime validity on disjoint randomized pairs pays a measured width price against all-pairs group-sequential methods; and a partial-score envelope halves decision time when slow episodes have certified partial outcomes. Code, manifests and derived tables are released.
