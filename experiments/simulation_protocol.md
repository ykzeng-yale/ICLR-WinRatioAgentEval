# Simulation protocol and provenance

Protocol implemented on 2026-09-17 before viewing numerical outputs. This is an internal frozen analysis plan, not public preregistration. Extensions motivated by results must be separately labeled.

Each comparison uses independent A and B arrivals from the same stationary population. Their order is exchangeable under randomized AB/BA order, so explicit order simulation is unnecessary in this initial stationary experiment. One pair costs two agent executions. Compliance and success are independent Bernoulli outcomes within arm; cost is independent lognormal, with log standard deviation 0.45. This simple mechanism isolates the statistical issues; realistic dependence is a required stress-test extension.

Hierarchy: compliance, success, cost. Absolute ties in binary outcomes; cost decisive only when both agents are compliant and successful and their difference exceeds 5% of the larger cost. Both unsuccessful trajectories receive no efficiency wins. Success comparisons are retained if both have the same compliance outcome, including both noncompliant; a later protocol sensitivity should assess an absorbing compliance-failure rule.

Six scenarios are defined in experiments/run_simulations.py: identical agents; cheaper equally effective agent; cheaper agent with success harm; cheaper agent with compliance harm; joint success and efficiency gain; weak success gain. Exact net-benefit targets are calculated analytically and independently checked with large Monte Carlo draws. All definitions and code hashes are saved in simulation_manifest.json.

Primary guarded decision: net benefit >0, success difference >-0.03, compliance difference >-0.01. These are illustrative margins, not expert-elicited operational requirements. Alpha=0.05 per gate for one conjunction under the stationary intersection-union null; this does not establish simultaneous individual 95% claims. Ternary bounded-score e-process is an equally weighted mixture of 40 fixed bets geometrically spaced between 0.0001 and 0.99/(1+c) for gate threshold c. No bets are fitted to outcomes. The finite grid guarantees validity but not consistency for every arbitrarily small positive gap.

Comparators: win-only betting (no component protection), guarded betting, guarded normal-mixture confidence sequence (rho=100 fixed), fixed-horizon guarded one-sided Wald intervals, repeated unadjusted guarded Wald intervals, ten equally spaced planned looks with Bonferroni-adjusted guarded Wald intervals. The Wald methods use estimated variance, so even their adjusted versions have only asymptotic justification; they are not presented as exact group-sequential win-statistic implementations. The win-only method targets preference and is not called statistically invalid when it accurately detects a composite gain in a harmful agent. Its result is classified as a deployment failure relative to the stated guardrails.

Initial run: 2,000 independent replications per scenario, maximum 10,000 pairs, monitoring from 100 pairs every 50 pairs with the final and ten group looks included. Exact e-process guarantees hold at any observation count, although this experiment samples the stated monitoring schedule. Rate uncertainty uses 95% Wilson intervals; mean capped stopping time reports Monte Carlo standard error. Nondeployment consumes the maximum sample size in this metric. Median stopping times among deployments are conditional summaries and must not be interpreted as unconditional sample savings.

Required next extensions: boundary-null guardrails, correlations and task heterogeneity, drifting running-average targets, randomized-order inverse weighting, dependent replicates and pseudoreplication, delayed completion bias, tuning robustness, larger terminal budgets, and an estimand-aligned efficient sequential U-statistic comparator where its assumptions apply. Record each as an extension rather than retrospectively relabeling it prespecified.
