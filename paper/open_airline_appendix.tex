\section{Interactive open-weight airline study: retained outcomes and replay scope}
\label{app:open_airline}
Fresh local open-weight outputs in $\tau^2$-bench airline
\citep{tau2bench2025} follow prospectively specified batch collection,
all A then all B, with a prespecified replay schedule. Physical exposure
was not randomized into replay order. We retain every planned unit and
distinguish recorded outcomes from incomplete resource accounting.

\paragraph{Systems, tasks and configuration.}
The roster comprises tasks 1--49 with two trial seeds, 626729 and 373753,
reused across tasks and arms. Task 0 was excluded before collection
because it had been used in smoke runs. A uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct;
B uses Qwen3-4B-Instruct-2507. Both use Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct as the
user simulator. The quantized GGUF files use Q4\_K\_M; their repository
revisions and individual file hashes accompany the data. The runtime
is llama.cpp commit \texttt{4fea119de30f6a923992780f6fd5ccb0bee5d47d},
with a 32,768-token context and one server slot. The benchmark commit is
\texttt{b7ea9074c1cba482b30687fecdb5c8425fd6f619}.
Collection used an Apple M5 with 32 GiB memory, 100 maximum steps,
10 maximum errors, concurrency one, agent temperature 0.3 and user
temperature zero. A shared one serving process between agent and user;
B used separate processes for its two models. These serving and batch
differences belong to the measured systems and prevent attributing an
observed resource difference solely to model size.

\paragraph{Canonical outcomes and failed attempts.}
All 196 task--trial--arm units have one canonical record, with no duplicate
or absent planned units. There are 194 saved trajectories and two A
infrastructure-failure placeholders with empty messages, zero recorded
duration and missing reward. Both placeholders count as unsuccessful
planned units. Each arm has 15/98 successes (15.3\%). A terminates with
55 user stops, 27 step-limit stops, 14 too-many-error stops and two
infrastructure failures; B has 69 user stops and 29 step-limit stops.
Equal observed success counts do not establish equivalence or noninferiority.

Reward bases are present for 124 user-stop trajectories; 70 others have
termination-based zero reward without that basis. These are archived
benchmark labels, not 194 independently completed verification procedures.

The retry rule returns the first whole-simulation attempt that completes
without an exception, irrespective of reward, or a failure placeholder
after four unsuccessful attempts. The logs reconcile to 206 attempts:
194 produced retained trajectories and 12 were discarded, all in A.
Discarded causes are five context-window overflows, four JSON-decoding
failures after truncation, two request timeouts and one interrupted
attempt when the operator stopped the first invocation. A task 6/trial 0
had seven attempts across invocations; task 15/trial 0 had two and
task 32/trial 1 had four. All 30 retained successes arose from
single-attempt units. A completed low-reward unit was not rerun to
improve its outcome.

The attempt ledger supplies statuses and approximate log-derived timing,
not complete request-level token usage for discarded attempts. Zero
messages in a placeholder mean no saved message record; they do not mean
zero operational consumption. Accordingly, we report canonical token
counts with this explicit scope and do not claim an operational resource
advantage, failure-inclusive cost, or elapsed-time saving. Server counters
give a lower bound of 246,284 additional A-collection generated tokens
absent from the canonical records; their agent/user partition is unknown.
This combines 198,280 tokens from cancelled-request progress counters
with 48,004 completed-request tokens after excluding smoke calls and
retained messages. It remains an incomplete usage total. Saved-message
agent and user prompt/completion counts are separately available in the
supplement. Different model tokenizers further limit compute comparisons.

\paragraph{Amendment and chronology.}
Five A units were completed before an operational failure triggered an
amendment adding a 1,024-token completion cap for both roles and arms,
and a 1,800-second simulation timeout. Those five units remain in the
denominator; the other 191 canonical records belong to the amended
invocation. The operator had seen the first five A outcomes and failure
information, but no B outcomes. This was not fully outcome-blind.
The five completed trajectories did not reach the later caps; invariance
of their outcomes under the changed serving regime was not tested.

The original amendment's 20:45 UTC decision time was an incorrect clock
estimate and is preserved with an explicit erratum. The amended invocation
started at 20:33:06 UTC and contains the amended commands. The owner's
reported file modification time shortly before that start is not an
independent immutable decision timestamp. The first invocation's log
snapshot records two failed attempts followed by an interrupted third
attempt on task 6, correcting the initial report of only two attempts.
Both original and amended runner versions were recovered from source
history and matched exactly to their respective invocation-manifest hashes.
The combined A raw file retains first-invocation top-level configuration
metadata, so per-unit regime flags and invocation commands take precedence
over treating that field as a uniform policy for every A record.

\paragraph{Descriptive contrasts.}
The hierarchy prioritizes success, then agent completion tokens with a
5\% larger-value relative tolerance, then assistant tool calls with zero
tolerance. Resource tiers require joint success; joint failure ties.
Positive signs favor B. The 49 scheduled cross-arrival replay pairs give
10 B wins, 30 ties and nine losses, or $1/49=0.02041$ net benefit.
All 19 non-ties are decided by success. Averaging all four A/B trial
combinations within each of the 49 tasks gives 28 wins, 141 ties and
27 losses across 196 dependent comparisons, or $1/196=0.00510$.
Matched-trial and different-trial descriptions are $0.01020$ and $0$,
respectively. None of these comparison counts is an independent sample
size for a binomial or task-population interval.

The two trial seeds are reused across all tasks; task clustering alone
does not establish independence from common seed, period or machine
effects. First-block task uniqueness is also insufficient. We therefore
omit contributed task-$t$, independent-arm Welch, fixed-mean betting
and win-ratio intervals rather than asserting their assumptions.
No retained sensitivity analysis is selected for a favorable interval.

\paragraph{A conditional observed-array illustration.}
To illustrate the observation protocol without claiming new-task inference,
condition on the complete retained A/B outcome array and the matching.
For pair $k$, let $z_k(0)$ and $z_k(1)$ be the two computable signs under
the alternative orientations. Under a model of independent fair replay
coins, with collection, amendment and retention independent of those
coins, $\mu_k=\{z_k(0)+z_k(1)\}/2$. The pre-pair filtration contains
the array, matching and earlier revealed coins, but not future coins.
Task reuse and dependence within the conditioned array do not require
independent task draws for this conditional calculation.

A post-hoc application of the fixed normal-mixture construction
($V_n=n$, $\rho=100$, $\alpha=0.05$) to the revealed signs gives a
final band $[-0.60932,0.65014]$ at 49 pairs, centered at $0.02041$ with
radius $0.62973$. The target $49^{-1}\sum_k\mu_k=0.01020$ is already
known from the complete array: the band illustrates masking, not uncertainty
about that array. Its fixed-construction guarantee assumes the stated coin
model, without post-hoc selection adjustment or inference over fresh model
runs, new tasks or a production population. No execution was stopped;
the replay establishes no deployment or stopping gain.

\paragraph{Reproduction scope.}
The supplement retains all metrics, 98 arrivals, 206 attempt rows, regime
flags, source/raw hashes and model identities. The builder recomputes
descriptions and replay bands without model calls. Projected data omit
dialogue, tool arguments, customer strings, tracebacks and identifiers;
archived reward labels are not independently readjudicated.
