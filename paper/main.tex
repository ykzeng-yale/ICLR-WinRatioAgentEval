\title{Guarded Win Statistics for Continuous Agent Evaluation}

\begin{abstract}
Agent evaluation combines verified completion, policy compliance, and
resource use, often while trajectories are still arriving. A hierarchical
win statistic makes outcome priorities explicit, but a favorable
aggregate can conceal a decline in a higher-priority population outcome.
We develop a guarded evaluation protocol that combines prioritized
comparisons with separate component requirements. It distinguishes
same-task benchmark repetitions from randomized cross-arrival online
comparisons and gives finite-sample continuous-monitoring guarantees.
For delayed outcomes, feasible completions of partial traces bound the
final pair score. A prefix envelope transfers established full-data
guarantees to these partial scores and cannot decide later than its
matched completed-prefix comparator. Complete proofs identify the
required enrollment-order assumptions and distinguish threshold error
control from a calendar-time e-process. In 2,000-repetition stationary
simulations, guarded betting made false deployments in 0.45\% of
identical-agent runs versus 31.05\% for repeated ordinary Wald tests.
In a separate informative-delay study, partial evidence reduced mean
capped decision time from 41.285 to 22.363 ticks for an equally successful,
cheaper alternative; both valid methods deployed in 99.7\% of runs and
had 0.6\% null false deployments. An ordinal replay of 10,008
historical trace comparisons certified 34.2\% before the later terminal
marker, demonstrating archived certificate feasibility without claiming operational
latency savings. Historical comparisons also expose success losses
hidden by aggregate wins. These
results support explicit decisions for a specified workload and
preference rule, with unresolved outcomes retained in the analysis.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
An agent can finish a task while using excessive resources, violate a
permission rule while obtaining a correct answer, or add verification
steps that improve reliability at a higher cost. An evaluator therefore
has several outcomes, often with a meaningful order of importance.
Accuracy--cost analyses emphasize that capability alone does not
characterize an agent system \citep{kapoor2024agents}. Interactive agent
benchmarks further expose variation across repeated trajectories and
across task families \citep{tau2bench2025,sweagent2024}. These measurements
leave a consequential statistical question: when does accumulating
evidence justify replacing the deployed system?

Generalized pairwise comparisons and win statistics address ordered
outcomes in clinical trials \citep{buyse2010generalized,pocock2012win}.
A comparison proceeds through a prespecified hierarchy, stopping at the
first meaningful difference. Applied to agents, the rule might prefer
policy compliance, then verified completion, then lower cost among
successful runs. Unlike a weighted score, the explanation of a win
identifies the deciding tier. This construction is established statistical
machinery, and win statistics have already appeared in AI evaluation
\citep{realpocqi2026}.

Three distinctions determine whether such a comparison can support an
online decision. First, priority within a pair does not protect a
population-level component: many cost wins can outweigh fewer success
losses. Second, a resettable benchmark observes both systems on a common
task, whereas an ordinary live A/B experiment observes only the assigned
system on each arrival. The corresponding preferences need not agree.
Third, repeatedly inspecting a fixed-sample confidence interval does not
preserve its error rate. Repeated runs, adaptive allocation, and delayed
completion add further ways for the nominal comparison to differ from the
one actually estimated.

We specify a protocol that keeps these decisions separate and then
combines them. The inferential ingredients are existing bounded-score
confidence sequences and betting processes
\citep{howard2021confidence,waudbysmith2024betting}. Our contribution is a
design-specific treatment of prioritized agent evaluation: explicit
targets for paired and online data; a guarded deployment rule with the
appropriate stationary or drifting error guarantee; partial-trace score bounds that retain pending episodes; and reproducible
experiments examining compensation, informative delay, and dependence. We give
complete derivations, including conditions under which the guarantees do
not apply. We do not propose a new generic confidence-sequence inequality
or claim that sequential win statistics are new.

\section{What is a win, and whose outcomes are compared?}
\label{sec:estimands}
Let $Y$ record an entire episode through a fixed evaluation horizon:
verified outcomes, selected trajectory summaries, and resources. A
chronological tool call is not an independent statistical unit. For tier
$k$, an antisymmetric rule $d_k(y,y')\in\{-1,0,1\}$ reports a meaningful
advantage, disadvantage, or operational tie. The hierarchical kernel is
\begin{equation}
 h(y,y')=\sum_{k=1}^{K}d_k(y,y')\prod_{\ell<k}
             \ind\{d_\ell(y,y')=0\}.
 \label{eq:main_kernel}
\end{equation}
Only the first decisive tier contributes. A resource comparison can be
disabled unless both episodes satisfy a symmetric eligibility rule, such
as both achieving verified completion. For positive costs, our empirical
primary tolerance is $|c_A-c_B|>0.05\max(c_A,c_B)$. Equality is a tie.
This threshold is an operational preference, not a significance test.

For a specified comparison law, let $(p_W,p_L,p_T)$ denote win, loss, and
tie probabilities. We monitor the bounded net benefit
$\theta=p_W-p_L=\E h$. We also report $\mathrm{WR}=p_W/p_L$ when $p_L>0$,
and $\mathrm{WO}=(1+\theta)/(1-\theta)$ when defined. A win ratio of two
does not imply twice the task-success probability. Tier-level win and
loss contributions accompany the summary, together with the component
means. Zero losses do not justify adding an arbitrary pseudocount.

\paragraph{Three comparison targets.}
For task description $X$ and episode randomness $\xi$, a resettable
benchmark can estimate
\begin{equation}
 \theta_{\mathrm{task}}=\E_X\E_{\xi_A,\xi_B}
 h\{Y^A(X,\xi_A),Y^B(X,\xi_B)\},\qquad
 \xi_A\perp\xi_B\mid X.
 \label{eq:main_task}
\end{equation}
This compares independent runs of the same task and depends on the task
distribution. Using the same simulator seed instead defines a coupled-run
preference. Neither is automatically the individual live-arrival
cross-world quantity $\E h(Y^A,Y^B)$, which requires a joint
potential-outcome law unobserved under single-system exposure.
The distinction between marginal, individual, and same-covariate
preferences is established in \citet{even2025rethinking}.
Contrast functions averaged over the marginal potential-outcome laws,
with inverse-probability and doubly robust estimation, are developed by
\citet{mao2018causal}; this distributional causal target is not new here.

For independent online arrivals in stratum $s$, the observable
distributional comparison is
\begin{equation}
 \theta_s=\int h(a,b)\,dF_{A,s}(a)\,dF_{B,s}(b).
 \label{eq:main_online}
\end{equation}
This can mix tasks of different difficulty within a stratum. Stratification
by an outcome-blind task family refines the question but does not make
separate users the same user. Appendix~\ref{app:theory} supplies an explicit
coupling example with identical arm marginals and opposite individual
preferences. The example illustrates an identification limitation; it is
not evidence that the product-law target in
Equation~\eqref{eq:main_task} is inherently unidentified.

\paragraph{Pairwise priority and population protection.}
Let $B$ always succeed. Suppose $A$ fails with probability $\epsilon$,
and otherwise succeeds more cheaply. Then a success-first hierarchy gives
$\theta=1-2\epsilon$ while
$\Delta_S=P(S^A=1)-P(S^B=1)=-\epsilon$.
Thus even a strongly favorable preference can violate a success
noninferiority margin. We define the desired deployment region as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal D=\{\theta>0,\ \Delta_j> -\delta_j\text{ for all }j=1,\ldots,J\},
 \label{eq:main_region}
\end{equation}
where $g_j(Y)\in[0,1]$ is an independently reported component and
$\Delta_j=\E g_j(Y^A)-\E g_j(Y^B)$. The margins $\delta_j$ require
application-specific justification. A relative compliance requirement
also does not certify an acceptable absolute event rate.

\section{A randomized online observation protocol}
\label{sec:online}
Before observing outcomes, form disjoint pairs of arrivals within a
prespecified stratum. Let $R_i=1$ assign system $A$ to the first arrival
and $B$ to the second; $R_i=0$ reverses the order. The known probability
$q_i=\Prob(R_i=1\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})$ may depend on completed past data,
but is bounded away from zero and one. Each arrival receives only one
system. This design fixes one exposure per arm per pair; it is not an
arbitrary traffic-allocation bandit.

Write $U_i=h(Y^A_{i1},Y^B_{i2})$ and
$V_i=h(Y^A_{i2},Y^B_{i1})$ for the potential oriented pair scores. Only
one is observed. The inverse-probability score
\begin{equation}
 Z_i=\frac{R_iU_i}{2q_i}+
          \frac{(1-R_i)V_i}{2(1-q_i)}
 \label{eq:main_ht}
\end{equation}
has conditional expectation $\E\{(U_i+V_i)/2\mid\mathcal F_{i-1}\}$
under randomization independent of the potential records and no
cross-arrival interference. With conditionally independent, exchangeable
arrivals in a stratum, this reduces to
Equation~\eqref{eq:main_online}. Otherwise it remains a symmetric
cross-arrival target, rather than silently imposing exchangeability.
Component scores replace $h$ by $g_j(a)-g_j(b)$.

For balanced order randomization, $q_i=1/2$ and $Z_i\in\{-1,0,1\}$.
For unbalanced randomized order, $|Z_i|\leq
B_i=1/\{2\min(q_i,1-q_i)\}$. Unweighted observed preferences can be
biased when the two positions differ. The target, weighting, and range
must therefore be specified together. The proof is a conditional
randomization identity (Theorem~\ref{thm:pair_id}), not an assumption that
adaptive allocation makes potential outcomes observable.

\paragraph{Endpoint maturity.}
Freeze an episode horizon $H$. Prespecified per-episode limits define
endpoint outcomes; exhaustion of a study-level monetary budget instead
leaves unobserved endpoints requiring bounds. A baseline processes the completely matured prefix of enrolled pairs.
This can incur latency when a slow earlier pair blocks later completed
pairs. It preserves the order defining the target. Selecting whichever
runs finish first can preferentially include fast successes and change
the comparison law. A display at a random completed-prefix index is
covered by an all-prefix guarantee; this is not a license to delete
missing or slow episodes. The delayed-outcome result in
Proposition~\ref{prop:delay} states the necessary filtration assumptions.

\section{Continuous monitoring and guarded decisions}
\label{sec:monitoring}
\paragraph{A finite-sample bound for drifting averages.}
Let $\mu_i=\E(Z_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})$ and
$\bar\mu_n=n^{-1}\sum_{i\leq n}\mu_i$. If the predictable range of $Z_i$
has width $r_i$, put $V_n=\sum_{i\leq n}r_i^2/4$. For a fixed
$\rho>0$, a normal-mixture confidence sequence is
\begin{equation}
 \bar Z_n\ \pm\ \frac{1}{n}
 \sqrt{(V_n+\rho)\log\!\left(\frac{V_n+\rho}{\rho\alpha^2}\right)}.
 \label{eq:main_cs}
\end{equation}
Simultaneously over all $n$, this interval covers $\bar\mu_n$ with
probability at least $1-\alpha$. For balanced ternary scores $V_n=n$.
The proof integrates Hoeffding exponential supermartingales against a
normal mixing law and applies Ville's inequality
\citep{howard2021confidence}. Under drift the covered quantity is a
running average of conditional effects; it does not describe the current
or future deployment population. Range-only bounds can be conservative
for rare-event components.

\paragraph{A directional betting implementation for stationary gates.}
For a bounded score $X_i\in[-B,B]$, gate threshold $c>-B$, and fixed
bets $0<\lambda_k<1/(B+c)$ with weights $w_k\geq0$ summing to one,
define
\begin{equation}
 E_n(c)=\sum_k w_k\prod_{i=1}^n\{1+\lambda_k(X_i-c)\}.
 \label{eq:main_bet}
\end{equation}
Under the conditional null $\E(X_i\mid\mathcal F_{i-1})\leq c$, this
is a nonnegative supermartingale starting at one. Crossing $1/\alpha$
therefore rejects the gate null with anytime error at most $\alpha$
\citep{waudbysmith2024betting}. Our stationary simulations use 40 fixed
geometric bets and ternary count sufficient statistics. Finite-grid
validity does not imply power for arbitrarily small gaps: at least one
bet must have positive expected log growth. This implementation is a
standard betting baseline specialized to the deployment rule.

\paragraph{One deployment claim versus simultaneous component claims.}
Let $c_0=0$ for preference and $c_j=-\delta_j$ for the component gates.
Under constant conditional means, accept deployment only after every gate
has valid evidence against its null. If the deployment region fails,
at least one fixed component null is true. A false deployment entails a
crossing of that component's level-$\alpha$ process. Consequently the
probability of ever falsely deploying is at most $\alpha$, without
dividing $\alpha$ across the required conjuncts
(Theorem~\ref{thm:iut}). This intersection--union guarantee concerns the
single deployment decision; it is not simultaneous $1-\alpha$ coverage
for all reported effects.

Under arbitrary drift, the component violating the region can change
over time. Then a fixed-null argument is insufficient. Use simultaneous
component confidence sequences, for example at levels
$\alpha/(J+1)$, and deploy only when every lower bound for its running
target exceeds its threshold (Theorem~\ref{thm:drift_gate}). For several
candidates or repeated model revisions, further error allocation is
necessary. Reusing the same data to choose the hierarchy, tolerances,
or candidate invalidates a guarantee that assumes those choices frozen.

\subsection{Using incomplete trajectories without selecting fast outcomes}
\label{sec:partial}
Let $N(t)$ denote the number of enrolled pairs at calendar time $t$.
For each gate, the observed partial traces define bounds
$\ell_i(t)\leq X_i\leq u_i(t)$ on the final fixed-horizon score,
within the known score range, simultaneously for every pair and update.
These bounds range over every
feasible completion. For a success-then-cost hierarchy, a successful
$A$ episode at cost 1 already defeats a pending $B$ episode that has
irreversibly spent 2: $B$ either fails or succeeds at higher cost.
An unresolved higher safety tier would prevent that certificate.
An absence of observed violations alone cannot certify final compliance.

Write $b_\alpha(V_n)$ for the numerator of the radius in
Equation~\eqref{eq:main_cs}. For every enrolled prefix $n\leq N(t)$,
\begin{equation}
 \left[\frac{\sum_{i\leq n}\ell_i(t)-b_\alpha(V_n)}{n},
       \frac{\sum_{i\leq n}u_i(t)+b_\alpha(V_n)}{n}\right]
 \label{eq:main_partial_cs}
\end{equation}
covers the corresponding running conditional mean simultaneously over
$n$ and $t$ with probability at least $1-\alpha$. On the event that
the complete-score confidence sequence covers every prefix, replacing
its score sum by lower and upper bounds preserves coverage pathwise.
No outcome-independent reveal-time assumption is needed. Its width is
the complete-data width plus $n^{-1}\sum_i(u_i-\ell_i)$, making the
cost of unresolved information explicit. If the number enrolled tends to infinity and that average width vanishes,
stationary means with strictly positive gate margins are eventually
resolved (Appendix~\ref{app:asynchronous}).

For stationary gates, positive betting factors similarly give
\begin{equation}
 \underline E_{n,t}(c)=\sum_k w_k\prod_{i\leq n}
            \{1+\lambda_k(\ell_i(t)-c)\}\ \leq\ E_n(c).
 \label{eq:main_partial_wealth}
\end{equation}
Thus a partial-score crossing at any time and prefix entails a crossing
of the latent complete-score process, controlling its probability under
the fixed conditional null. Retrospectively refining a pending factor
uses new information about the original pair; it does not add a new
observation. Under stationarity, deploy if one available prefix satisfies
all gates. Evaluating every candidate prefix includes every completed
prefix and therefore cannot decide later than a completed-prefix method
using the same candidates, bets, and calendar looks. Using only the
latest enrolled prefix lacks this dominance, because additional pending
pairs can dilute its lower wealth.

The argument assumes a valid complete-score enrollment filtration and
guaranteed completion bounds. Shared infrastructure, candidate adaptation,
or fallible interim labels can violate those premises. We prove
threshold-crossing control, not that the partial wealth is an e-process
in the richer calendar-time filtration. Worst-case pending-outcome
stopping and time-uniform filtration transfer have direct precedents
\citep{henzi2022forecast,henzi2022correction,choe2024filtrations}; our
specialization uses hierarchy and component bounds in a deployment
protocol. Under drift, each selected prefix retains its own target and
requires simultaneous component coverage. A maximum of historical lower
bounds is not a lower bound on the current drifting effect.

\paragraph{Offline repeated runs.}
With $R$ common-seed trials for each task, the $R^2$ cross-run scores are
dependent. Under independent seed clusters, pairs with different seed
indices estimate the independent-run target; the $R$ same-seed pairs
estimate a coupled target. Their equal-weight mixture assigns weight
$1/R$ to the latter. We average eligible cross-run scores within each
task, weight tasks equally, and resample complete tasks for uncertainty.
The effective independent units are tasks, or larger shared clusters
when required. The appendix provides the unbiased task estimator,
asymptotic variance, bounded-mean alternative, and coupling decomposition.

\section{Related work and scope}
Generalized pairwise comparisons \citep{buyse2010generalized} and the
clinical win ratio \citep{pocock2012win} establish the ordered-outcome
construction. Clustered win-statistic inference is also developed
\citep{fang2026cluster}; benchmark tasks with both systems observed are
not parallel-arm randomized clinical clusters. Sequential derived-win
designs \citep{zhang2024sequential,bergemann2026group} and recent
anytime-valid U-statistic methods \citep{cai2026ustatistics} are direct
methodological precedents. \citet[Section~4.2]{manole2023martingale}
also describes a finite-sample confidence-sequence route for bounded symmetric
U-statistics under i.i.d. sampling using reverse martingales. Its generic
displayed construction is one-sample; it is not directly our two-arm
antisymmetric kernel. Our disjoint-pair construction trades some
information available to all-pairs estimators for a simple randomized
score and explicit finite-sample monitoring. We do not establish its
efficiency relative to every sequential U-statistic method.

Two recent win-statistic proposals address nearby concerns.
\citet{li2026worstcase} tests benefit under every prespecified hierarchy
or threshold rule using an intersection--union argument and asymptotic
fixed-sample inference. Our conjunction uses separate outcome-component
requirements and continuously monitored evidence; the intersection--union
principle itself is established. \citet{mccoy2026psnb} replaces
reach-weighted tier aggregation with prespecified weights on
stage-conditional effects, explicitly limiting lower-tier influence.
That changes the composite estimand; it does not impose our requirements
on raw population success or compliance differences. A positive
reweighted composite can still fail such a requirement, as illustrated
in Appendix~\ref{app:pairing_efficiency}. We claim neither discovery of
lower-tier dominance nor superiority to these different decision targets.

Anytime off-policy confidence sequences already support deployment gates
\citep{karampatziakis2021offpolicy}. Combining superiority with
noninferiority guardrails, including their intersection--union error
logic, is established in product experimentation
\citep{schultzberg2024riskaware}. Our use of those ideas is specific to
prioritized episode comparisons and component constraints. The
agent-evaluation contribution must therefore be judged by the clarity
of the design, the empirical consequences it reveals, and the usefulness
of the complete protocol, rather than novelty of e-processes themselves.
Delayed-outcome inference also depends on the target:
\citet{lindon2026delayed} studies calendar-time cumulative rewards,
whereas our bounds retain fixed-horizon enrollment-prefix outcomes.
Historical benchmark outcomes remain measurements under their original
harness and model configurations. A favorable comparison does not
identify the module causing a gain or establish transport to production.

\citet{huang2026leaderboard} already examines estimand-aware pairwise
conclusions and utility-sensitive selections on public agent records,
including SWE-bench and $\tau_2$-bench. Our additional focus is the
hierarchical contrast under randomized observation, component constraints,
and certified partial-outcome monitoring. We do not claim that
target-dependent rankings or resource-sensitive selection are new.

\paragraph{Limitations.}
The primary synthetic model deliberately isolates compensation and
monitoring; real workflows have richer dependence, measurement error,
and workload shifts. Benchmark cost records are historical charges, not
current price quotes. An imperfect success or policy grader changes the
measured target, and rare severe failures require separate monitoring.
Our component margins are illustrative, not elicited from application
owners. All-pairs methods may use data more efficiently than disjoint
pairs. Partial bounds can remain uninformative when outcomes cannot be
certified early; our delay simulation uses one deliberately simple
measurement pipeline and two outcome scenarios. The historical prefix
audit validates certificate feasibility under an artificial message
schedule; its operational latency and deployment value remain untested. Public
retrospective traces and laboratory experiments do not constitute a
randomized intervention among production users.

\section*{Reproducibility statement}
The supplementary code contains the comparator, monitoring procedures,
simulation generators, analytic simulation targets, seed and source
manifests, and result tables. Appendix~\ref{app:theory} states the
assumptions and complete proofs. Public-data analysis records original
artifact locations, hashes, processing rules, and task-level uncertainty.

\section*{Ethics statement}
This work studies synthetic environments and public benchmark records.
Deployment rules require externally justified preferences and safeguards;
a favorable composite cannot certify absolute safety or fairness.
Released materials exclude credentials and private user data. No live
production users are assigned experimental agents in the reported
historical reanalyses or synthetic studies.
