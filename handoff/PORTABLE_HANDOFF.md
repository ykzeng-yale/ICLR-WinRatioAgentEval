## Research question and contribution

The project asks how to compare stochastic AI agents when success, safety/quality, trajectory evidence and resource use have an **explicit priority order**, rather than a single unqualified win rate. A task-matched offline estimand compares agents on the same task; a prospective online A/B estimand compares randomized exposure across arrivals. The paper develops prespecified pairwise win statistics and inference, explains ties and priority rules, and treats continuously monitored online decisions as requiring both a statistical decision rule and component guardrails. It separates delayed/asynchronous observations, task/population shifts, sequential looks, abstention and failure accounting. Established generalized pairwise-comparison, win-ratio and martingale work is credited; the novelty claim is the scoped agent-evaluation formulation and checked combination, **not** “first use of wins in AI.” See [`arxiv/source/main.tex`](../arxiv/source/main.tex), [`arxiv/source/theory.tex`](../arxiv/source/theory.tex), [`evidence/venue_and_novelty.md`](../evidence/venue_and_novelty.md), and [`evidence/arxiv_experiment_plan.md`](../evidence/arxiv_experiment_plan.md).

The current first-preprint claims are bounded by their designs. Synthetic streams and historical replay do not become a fresh randomized production experiment. Open-weight coding and airline results are narrow observed collections; their post-hoc running-mean or masked-replay illustrations do not establish a prospective stopping effect. Do not infer equivalence from a zero observed success difference, treat a conditional zero-difference planning horizon as a theorem of universal abstention, or require a favorable result for study completion. Preserve unfavorable outcomes, attempts, failures and missingness.
