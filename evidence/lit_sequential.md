# Literature sweep: anytime-valid / sequential inference for online A/B tests and pairwise (win/tie/loss) data

Sweep date: 2026-09-17 (America/New_York). Scope: continuous-monitoring inference for A/B tests, multinomial and pairwise outcomes, design-based sequential inference, delayed outcomes, and multi-metric (guardrail) decisions. This extends `venue_and_novelty.md` and `theory_design.md`; it does not repeat their entries on Zhang–Wu, Bergemann–Hanson, Cai–Hu–Li, Even–Josse, or Fang et al. except where a new fact is added.

Verification convention. "Verified" means the abstract page, full text, proceedings page or documentation page was opened in this sweep. Statements marked UNVERIFIED come from search snippets, secondary pages or memory and must not be cited as fact. BibTeX keys refer to `paper/refs_sequential.bib`. Assessments in the last column or in "Implication" lines are ours, not the authors'.

## 1. Headline answers to the four focus questions

**Q1. Does anyone have an anytime-valid confidence sequence for the win ratio, or for functionals of a win/tie/loss multinomial?**

- No source opened in this sweep gives a confidence sequence explicitly labelled as being for the win ratio, win odds or net benefit. Searches on "win ratio" combined with e-value / e-process / anytime-valid / confidence sequence / always valid returned only the clinical win-ratio literature (fixed-sample or group-sequential) and generic e-value material. The group-sequential (planned looks, asymptotic) literature on win statistics and the Mann–Whitney parameter is well developed: Nowak, Mütze & Konietschke (SMMR 2022) [nowak2022groupsequential], Wu et al. (Statistics in Medicine 2025) for the two-sample MWW parameter θ = P(X<Y)+½P(X=Y) [wu2025groupsequential], Zhang & Wu (SMMR 2025) for paired ordinal outcomes via a group-sequential Wilcoxon signed-rank U-statistic [zhang2025pairedordinal], plus Zhang–Wu 2024 and Bergemann–Hanson 2026 already in `theory_design.md`. Cai, Hu & Li 2026 (already recorded) is the closest generic *asymptotic* anytime-valid U-statistic result.
- **What Lindon & Malek (NeurIPS 2022) actually give** [lindon2022multinomial], from the full text (arXiv v2, 18 pages, read locally):
  - Data: x_1, x_2, ... i.i.d. Multinomial(1, θ), θ in the (d−1)-simplex. Null M0: θ = θ0 (point null). Alternative M1: θ ~ Dirichlet(α0). Their recommended informative choice is α0,i = k θ0,i for a concentration k > 0; α0,i = 1 gives the uniform prior.
  - Bayes factor (their Eq. 3): BF10(x_{1:n}) = Beta(α0 + S_n) / Beta(α0) · 1 / θ0^{S_n}, where S_n is the vector of category counts, Beta(v) = ∏Γ(v_i)/Γ(∑v_i), and θ0^{S_n} = ∏ θ0,i^{S_n,i}. They credit the closed form to Good (1967). Recursive form (Eq. 4): O_n(θ0) = [Beta(α_{n−1} + x_n)/Beta(α_{n−1})] · θ0^{−x_n} · O_{n−1}(θ0), with α_n = α_{n−1} + x_n, O_0 = 1.
  - Theorem 2.1: O_n(θ0) is a nonnegative martingale under M0 w.r.t. the canonical filtration. Theorem 2.2 (Ville): P_{θ0}(∃n: O_n(θ0) ≥ 1/u) ≤ u. Sequential p-value p_n = min(p_{n−1}, 1/O_n(θ0)). Theorem 2.3: n^{−1} log O_n(θ0) → D_KL(θ‖θ0) a.s. when θ ≠ θ0 (power one).
  - Theorem 2.4 (confidence sequence): C_n(u) = {θ in simplex : O_n(θ) < 1/u} satisfies P_θ(θ ∈ C_n(u) for all n) ≥ 1−u. C_n(u) is convex (concavity of the multinomial log-likelihood). Corollary 2.5 projects onto coordinates: the per-cell bounds j±_{n,i}(u) are obtained by maximizing/minimizing θ_i subject to c + log u ≤ ∑_i S_n,i log θ_i and ∑θ_i = 1, with c = log Beta(α0 + S_n) − log Beta(α0) (their Eq. 11). Coverage is simultaneous over all cells and all n.
  - Two-group/A/B use (Section 3): each arriving unit is assigned to arm i with probability ρ_i; only Bernoulli successes are recorded; success probability p_i(t) = e^{μ(t)} e^{δ_i} with a common time-varying nuisance μ(t). The arm of the next success is Multinomial(1, θ) with θ_i = ρ_i e^{δ_i} / ∑_j ρ_j e^{δ_j} (Eq. 12), independent of μ(t), so the multinomial CS transfers to δ through the ρ-softmax inverse (Theorem 3.1), and Corollary 3.2 gives simultaneous CSs for all contrasts ∑ a_i δ_i with ∑ a_i = 0 by convex optimization (Eq. 14); sequential p-values for one-sided hypotheses on contrasts are also convex programs (Eq. 15). Equality of arms is the multinomial null θ0 = ρ, which is also their sample-ratio-mismatch test. Section 4 lifts the same construction to inhomogeneous Poisson processes via superposition (Theorem 4.1). Netflix signup-funnel case study: a winner declared in "approximately 1 week instead of 9".
  - **Implication for a win/tie/loss stream.** Because the disjoint-pair outcome (win, tie, loss) is exactly Multinomial(1, θ) with d = 3, Theorem 2.4 gives, essentially for free, a time-uniform convex confidence set for (p_W, p_T, p_L), and any functional of θ (net benefit p_W − p_L, win ratio p_W/p_L, win odds (p_W + ½p_T)/(p_L + ½p_T)) can be bounded by optimizing that functional over C_n(u): p_W − p_L is linear (a convex program), and the win ratio is quasi-linear in θ so its sup/inf over the convex set reduce to linear feasibility problems (a level-set argument). Lindon & Malek do not state this; they never mention ties, ordinal outcomes, or ratio functionals. This is the most direct existing route to a finite-sample CS for the win ratio and would be the natural "Dirichlet-mixture" baseline against the normal-mixture / betting bounded-score CSs in `theory_design.md`. Its weakness relative to a bounded-score CS is that it needs the i.i.d. multinomial model (no drift in θ); its strength is that it is exact, prior-tunable, and delivers all three win statistics from one set.
- Other partial matches. Arnold, Choe, Scarsini & Tsetlin, "Betting on Bets" (2026) [arnold2026stochasticdominance] build anytime-valid e-processes for first-order stochastic dominance from *paired* streams (X_t, Y_t) by betting on 1{X≤z} − 1{Y≤z} mixed over thresholds z, with GRO stake λ*(z) = (p(z)−q(z))/(p(z)+q(z)); explicitly recommended for ordinal outcomes and with an A/B-testing section, but the null is dominance of CDFs, not a win-probability functional. van Meer & Koning, "Real-time Program Evaluation using Anytime-valid Rank Tests" (2025) [vanmeer2025ranktests] give finite-sample sequential-rank e-values for exchangeability, not a CS for P(X>Y). Shekhar & Ramdas (IEEE TIT 2024, 70(2):1178–1203) and Podkopaev & Ramdas (NeurIPS 2023) test equality in distribution, not a prespecified preference functional.

**Q2. Design-based (randomization-only) sequential inference for paired designs.**

- Ham, Lindon, Tingley & Bojinov, "Design-Based Confidence Sequences: A General Approach to Risk Mitigation in Panel Experiments" (arXiv v4, Aug 2026; Management Science version listed on the arXiv page, DOI 10.1287/mnsc.2023.00492) [ham2024designbased]. Verified from the v4 PDF: potential outcomes fixed, only assignment random; estimand is a finite-sample "CTE" (causal effect over enrolled units/periods, allowing arbitrary carryover); estimator is a per-period Horvitz–Thompson difference τ̂_{t|t−1} (Assumption 2.1: propensities bounded away from 0/1 given the past); Theorem 2.1 gives its mean and a Neyman-type *upper-bound* variance with an unbiased estimator; Assumption 2.2 bounds potential outcomes by M; Theorem 3.2 is an exact (nonasymptotic) gamma-mixture CS whose width scales with M; Section 4 gives a design-based *asymptotic* CS in the sense of Waudby-Smith et al. (Definition 4.1) under a non-vanishing-variance Assumption 4.1. Covers A/B, time-series, switchback and panel designs, plus a variance-reduction step with covariates; three Netflix applications; would have stopped a 30,000-person experiment after 100 people. Paired AB/BA within-arrival randomization is not treated as a named case, but the framework (assignments adapted to the past, HT scores bounded by M/ε) contains it; this is what our Result 4 in `theory_design.md` instantiates. Our paper should say so explicitly rather than presenting design-based sequential HT inference as new.
- Lindon & Kallus, "Design-Based Anytime-Valid Inference for Randomized Experiments with Delayed Outcomes and Staggered Entry" (arXiv 2603.25971, v2 May 2026) [lindon2026delayed]: purely design-based, unit-level Bernoulli assignment, no paired/cluster designs (see Q3).
- Zampieri, "Sequential Randomization Tests Using e-values" (arXiv 2512.04366, v9 May 2026) [zampieri2026ert]: nonparametric e-RT that bets sequentially on the randomized assignment given outcomes; validity from randomization alone under the sharp null of no effect; binary, event-only and continuous endpoints; abstract does not mention paired/block designs or CSs. Single-author preprint; treat as a design-based sequential *test*, not estimation.
- Malenica, Guo, Gan & Konigorski, "Anytime-valid inference in N-of-1 trials" (arXiv 2309.07353) [malenica2023nof1]: design-based asymptotic CSs for within-person crossover sequences — the closest published analogue of a within-unit A-then-B / B-then-A design.
- Liang & Bojinov, Mixture Adaptive Design (arXiv 2311.05794 v4) [liang2024mad] and Molitor & Gold (arXiv 2506.20523 v4) [molitor2026madcovar]: asymptotically anytime-valid CSs for the ATE under bandit-plus-Bernoulli mixing; relevant precedent for our adaptive-orientation-probability extension (Result 4) and for the ε-floor requirement.
- Bibaut & Kallus, "Demystifying Inference After Adaptive Experiments", Annual Review of Statistics and Its Application 12:407–423 (2025) [bibaut2025demystifying]: survey of why adaptivity breaks naive inference and of reweighting fixes. UNVERIFIED beyond search snippet (journal page not opened).

**Q3. How delayed outcomes are handled in sequential A/B tests.**

- Lindon & Kallus 2026 [lindon2026delayed] is the only paper found that addresses delay *inside* the anytime-valid guarantee. Verified from the HTML: staggered entry; treatment can change whether, when and how large an outcome is; estimand is the sample cumulative reward by calendar time t under each arm; Proposition 4.8 shows the IPW *treatment-effect* error is not a martingale under any filtration (cross-arm covariance), while arm-specific errors are martingales under a single-arm event-time filtration F_t(w) (Theorem 4.4); Theorems 4.6/4.9 give asymptotic CSs per arm and a union-bound CS for the difference, and Proposition 4.12 shows the union bound can be narrower than a variance upper bound when the arms' variance clocks are imbalanced. Units not yet observed contribute zero by construction (right-censoring is part of the estimand); never-events are permanent censoring. Augmented IPW must "activate at the event time and remain constant thereafter" (severely restricting AIPW to oracle forms). Positioned against Ham et al. 2022, Bibaut–Kallus–Lindon 2022 and Lindon–Kallus AISTATS 2025.
- Lindon & Kallus, "Anytime-Valid A/B Testing of Counting Processes", AISTATS 2025, PMLR 258:3529–3537 [lindon2025counting], and the continuous-time confidence-process preprint (arXiv 2410.09282) [lindon2024poisson]: cumulative-rate confidence processes and a closed-form e-process for rate equality; the natural precedent for monitoring error/timeout counts as a guardrail stream.
- Lindon & Malek 2022 (above) handle the practical fact that "a Bernoulli fail must be inferred from the absence of a Bernoulli success" by modelling only successes as an inhomogeneous process and conditioning on the total, which makes the analysis invariant to a common time-varying rate but does not make it invariant to treatment-dependent delay.
- Ankargren, Frånberg & Schultzberg, "It's About Time: What A/B Test Metrics Estimate" (arXiv 2411.06150) [ankargren2024time]: cumulative versus windowed metrics with repeated measurements yield different estimands tied to intake and runtime; cumulative metrics can lose power despite more data. Supports our "running-average conditional effect under drift" caveat and the need to fix an outcome horizon.
- Industry documentation opened (Statsig, Eppo, GrowthBook, Spotify Confidence) contains no explicit treatment of delayed outcomes in the sequential guarantee. Statsig's sequential-testing page (verified) states that early decisions are underpowered and that early significance on some metrics does not guarantee power to detect regressions in others.
- Implication: our Result 9 (display only fully observed enrollment prefixes; completion-order selection and timeout discarding are not justified) is consistent with, and simpler than, Lindon–Kallus 2026, but Lindon–Kallus identify a sharper point we must acknowledge: with treatment-dependent delay the *pair-difference* error is not a martingale in the observer filtration; the enrollment-prefix rule avoids this only because it waits for both members of a pair. A comparison paragraph is required.

**Q4. How multiple guardrail metrics are combined with a primary metric in valid sequential decisions.**

- Schultzberg, Ankargren & Frånberg, "Risk-aware product decisions in A/B tests with multiple metrics" (arXiv 2402.11609, Spotify) [schultzberg2024riskaware]: success, guardrail, deterioration and quality metrics with different tests; guardrails use non-inferiority tests and need no multiplicity adjustment of α; type-II error must be corrected when non-inferiority/deterioration/quality tests are in the decision rule; Monte Carlo validation. Schultzberg, "Nobody Puts Bonferroni in a Corner" (arXiv 2604.09256) [schultzberg2026bonferroni]: guardrail/quality metrics follow intersection-union logic and cannot inflate the false-positive rate, so only success metrics enter the Bonferroni denominator; 1,296 Spotify experiments. Both are fixed-sample framings; neither states a sequential IUT theorem. Our Results 7–8 (stationary IUT vs. drift-time budget split) are therefore consistent with industry logic but add the sequential and drifting-gate distinctions, which we have not seen stated elsewhere.
- Spotify Confidence documentation (verified): offers group-sequential tests and "always-valid inference"; sequential tests are always enabled for rollouts and deterioration checks always use sequential tests. A search snippet (UNVERIFIED) says Confidence uses the Maharaj et al. boundary with Bonferroni across metrics.
- Eppo (verified): default is a slightly modified Howard et al. Eq. 14 normal-mixture CS, B = t^{−1/2} sqrt((t+ρ) log((t+ρ)/(ρ(1−c)^2))), ρ tuned with N_tune = 10,000; guardrail cutoffs are downside thresholds compared against the *lower bound* of the CS, with a yellow warning when the lower bound breaches the cutoff but is not significantly negative and advice to "consider not rolling out". No joint error statement. Note the structural match to our CS radius in `theory_design.md` Result 5.
- Statsig (verified): mSPRT following Zhao, Liu & Deb (arXiv 1905.10493) [zhao2019staged]; no multiple-testing or guardrail statement on the sequential page; a separate Statsig blog (search snippet, UNVERIFIED) defines guardrail MDE as the largest regression one is willing to miss.
- GrowthBook (verified): asymptotic CSs of Waudby-Smith et al. with tuning N* = 5,000 default; nothing on guardrails.
- Optimizely (verified support page): Stats Engine = sequential testing with false-discovery-rate control across goals/variations; the Pekelis–Walsh–Johari "New Stats Engine" whitepaper exists per Semantic Scholar/search but its content could not be opened (UNVERIFIED).
- Kohavi, Tang & Xu, *Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments* (Cambridge UP 2020) [kohavi2020trustworthy]: Chapter 7 (verified via the Cambridge chapter page, pp. 102–110, DOI 10.1017/9781108653985.010) defines the OEC as a weighted combination of goal/driver metrics, recommends about five key metrics, and lists guardrail metrics alongside goal, driver and diagnostic metrics. The book's dedicated guardrail chapter (trust-related vs organisational guardrails) is reported in secondary summaries only (UNVERIFIED chapter number).
- Statistical machinery for combining sequential evidence: Vovk & Wang (AoS 2021) [vovk2021evalues] (e-values merge by averaging; e-to-p calibration), Wang & Ramdas e-BH (JRSS-B 2022, 84(3):822–852) [wang2022ebh] (FDR control under arbitrary dependence, no correction), Cho, Gan & Kallus PEAK (ICML 2024) [cho2024peak] (composite hypotheses on the mean vector of W bounded streams with adaptive stream sampling, evidence averaged across streams rather than union-bounded; Theorems 1–4), Sandoval, Waudby-Smith & Jordan (arXiv 2603.17925) [sandoval2026multiarmed] (global null across arms, UCB-style arm selection). PEAK's convex-region formulation is the closest existing tool if we want a *single* e-process for "primary ≥ margin and every guardrail ≥ its floor" instead of separate gates; it is not framed as an IUT and its power theorem needs the misspecified arm sampled infinitely often.

## 5. What is still open (our assessment)

1. Corrected in Round 7: absence of win-statistic terminology is not a methodological gap. Existing bounded-mean CSs already cover ternary net benefit, and Lindon–Malek's multinomial set supplies functional bounds by projection. Manole–Ramdas (2023), Section 4.2, also describes finite-sample time-uniform U-statistic inference through reverse martingales for symmetric kernels under i.i.d. sampling. Its displayed generic construction is one-sample; applying it to a two-arm win kernel needs a justified reduction or extension. None of these generic confidence-sequence ingredients is claimed as new in the current paper.
2. No sequential IUT statement for guardrails that separates stationary from drifting gates, and no treatment of *which* gate is false changing over time; Spotify's papers are fixed-sample.
3. Delay: Lindon–Kallus 2026 covers unit-level Bernoulli assignment with treatment-dependent delay; paired enrollment with a fixed outcome horizon (our setting) is not covered by them, but our result must be compared with their impossibility result for the difference error.
4. Design-based sequential inference for AB/BA pairs is contained in Ham et al.'s framework; the paired-hierarchical-score instantiation and its variance is our contribution only if stated as such.
5. Interleaving gives an honest historical anchor for online pairwise ternary evaluation; we must explain why agentic tasks generally need disjoint-pair exposure instead.
