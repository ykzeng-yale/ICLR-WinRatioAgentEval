Extends `venue_and_novelty.md`, `theory_design.md` and `empirical_feasibility.md`; it does not repeat the win-statistics, sequential-inference and clinical-AI sources audited there (Buyse, Pocock, Even–Josse, Fang, Zhang–Wu, Bergemann–Hanson, Cai, Howard, Waudby-Smith, Karampatziakis, Real-POCQi, MAPS-LLM, GAPS-Agent).

## 1. Bottom line for positioning

1. **No opened source applies a prioritized (hierarchical) pairwise win statistic — win ratio, net benefit, win odds, DOOR — to LLM or agent comparison, offline or online.** Searches for "win ratio", "hierarchical composite", "prioritized outcomes", "DOOR", "lexicographic", "success first then cost", "tie-break" combined with LLM/agent evaluation returned only (a) the clinical-AI applications already audited (Real-POCQi, MAPS-LLM, GAPS-Agent), (b) unrelated pairwise-ranking work (Bradley–Terry variants), and (c) multi-dimensional agent frameworks (CLEAR, HAL, reliability science) that report dimensions side by side without a prioritized pairwise composite. This supports a "first prioritized-composite pairwise framework for agent comparison" claim only in the narrow sense; the broad "first hierarchical comparison of AI systems" claim is defeated by the clinical-AI precedents in `venue_and_novelty.md`.
2. **The agent-benchmark literature reports point estimates on binary success with almost no uncertainty quantification.** τ-bench, τ²-bench, Terminal-Bench, HAL, Anthropic's SWE-bench/TAU-bench posts and the HAL website report accuracies (and, for HAL/τ²/Efficient Agents, cost) without confidence intervals (VERIFIED for each, see §2). This is the vacuum the paper fills; it is also why the "error bars" literature (§5) is the natural methodological neighbour rather than the benchmark papers.
3. **Reliability across repeated trials is now a first-class metric (pass^k), but it is still a marginal per-system statistic, not a paired comparison.** τ-bench defines pass^k as E_task[C(c,k)/C(n,k)] (VERIFIED, arXiv HTML), τ²-bench runs 4 trials per task at temperature 0 (VERIFIED), Rabanser et al. (ICML 2026 per arXiv) decompose reliability into twelve metrics along consistency/robustness/predictability/safety (VERIFIED). None of these define a task-paired comparison between two systems or a sequential test. This is an opening: the win-score framework can consume repeated trials as replicate sets within a task cluster, as `empirical_feasibility.md` already plans.
4. **Cost-controlled evaluation is an accepted norm (Kapoor et al. TMLR 2025; HAL ICLR 2026), but its instrument is the Pareto frontier, which is silent on how to decide between two non-dominated systems and gives no inference.** HAL's site explicitly says agents can be "100x more expensive while only being 1% better" (VERIFIED) and reports no CIs. A prioritized pairwise score with valid inference is a direct, defensible answer to "which of two frontier points should ship". Reviewers from this community will ask why not just show the Pareto plot; the paper needs the decision-relevance evidence listed in `venue_and_novelty.md` §"What remains open".
5. **"Guardrail metrics" is standard online-experimentation vocabulary (Kohavi–Tang–Xu 2020), and anytime-valid confidence sequences are already deployed in industrial A/B platforms (Adobe, WWW'23).** The paper should present guarded sequential deployment as the *known* experimentation pattern and position its contribution as the pairwise-prioritized estimand plus the identification analysis, not as inventing guardrails or sequential A/B testing. LLM-evaluation vendor blogs (GrowthBook, LangWatch, Statsig) use "guardrail" informally without any pairwise or prioritized composite (VERIFIED).
6. **The pairwise-preference statistics community has moved to richer models — ties (Ameli et al. ICLR 2025), per-prompt coefficients (P2L), style covariates (style control), rank-sets with coverage (Chatzi et al. NeurIPS 2024), conformal Elo, pluralistic top-k stability — all built on Bradley–Terry over crowdsourced *human preference* labels.** Our setting differs in two ways to stress: outcomes are *measured* (verifier success, cost, latency) rather than judged, and the score is a prespecified hierarchy over those outcomes rather than an elicited preference. The BT literature is a natural baseline for the "why not fit BT to the win/loss/tie labels" question; Ameli's factored tie model is the closest formal treatment of ties.
7. **Anytime-valid LLM evaluation has arrived in 2026 (Kotawala ICML-W; CELEUS; CITE; Wu–Nair–Candès), but all of it targets single-system accuracy or a paired Bernoulli difference, not a bounded hierarchical win score with guardrails.** These must be cited as contemporaneous work and distinguished by estimand, not by machinery.
8. **Measurement error in LLM-judge pipelines is a live reviewer concern** (Messing 2026: naive SEs 40–60% too small; Landesberg 2026: 67% pairwise ties from coarse judge scores; Comparative Trap; LLMBar). Using verifier-based success and measured cost/latency as the top tiers, and keeping any judged tier lowest with a noise-robust estimand, is the defensible design.

Takeaways specific to our design:

- **Success is almost always binary and verifier-defined** (DB state, unit tests, execution scripts, exact match). This is good for the top tier of the hierarchy: no judge noise. τ-bench's authors note final-state grading can miss policy violations (already recorded in `venue_and_novelty.md`); Kirgis et al. 2026 (§6) quantify how much log analysis changes conclusions.
- **Repeated trials exist only in τ/τ² (and some HAL runs)**; most benchmarks are single-attempt. Our offline case studies should be explicit that four trials per task is the ceiling of what released data support.
- **Cost is reported by τ, τ², HAL, Efficient Agents; latency by none of the opened benchmark papers.** `empirical_feasibility.md` already flags that τ² `duration` includes the simulator. A latency tier will need caveats or fresh runs.

## 9. How accepted ICLR evaluation-methodology papers are built (what reviewers rewarded)

Verified accepted examples: τ-bench (ICLR 2025 poster), WildBench (ICLR 2025), MLE-bench (ICLR 2025 oral), Model Equality Testing (ICLR 2025), Ameli et al. (ICLR 2025 per arXiv), HAL (ICLR 2026), Stroebl et al. (ICLR 2026), LLMs Get Lost (ICLR 2026 Outstanding). Common structure observed across the opened abstracts and proceedings pages:

1. **A crisp mismatch between current practice and real use** stated in one sentence (single-turn vs multi-turn; accuracy without cost; final-state grading without reliability; which model an API serves).
2. **A new measurement instrument with a named metric** (pass^k, WB-Reward with five outcomes, medal thresholds, MMD string-kernel test, factored tie model) defined formally and cheaply computable.
3. **Scale that makes the finding unarguable** (200K conversations; 21,730 rollouts/$40K; 240K votes; 31 endpoints tested), plus at least one surprising empirical fact (higher reasoning effort lowers accuracy; 11/31 endpoints differ; pass^8 < 25%).
4. **External validity evidence** (agreement with human rankings 0.95–0.98; expert-rater agreement; reproduction of leaderboard results).
5. **Released artifact** (benchmark, harness, package, logs).
6. Statistical-methodology acceptances at ICLR (Gao; Ameli) paired a clean hypothesis-testing or modelling formulation with a practically important application and open-source code; neither claimed new probability theory.

Implication: our paper needs (2) a named, formally defined decision statistic with guarantees; (3) real agent runs at a scale that produces at least one surprising, decision-relevant disagreement between Pareto/scalar/success-only rankings and the guarded hierarchical decision; (4) evidence the hierarchy tracks an external criterion (e.g., human deployment judgments or downstream user outcomes) or at least sensitivity analyses; (5) a package plus protocol.

## 10. Novelty threats (what a reviewer could say is "already done")

- **Kotawala 2026**: paired, clustered, anytime-valid LLM comparison with resolution diagnostics. Differs in estimand (paired accuracy difference), data (static leaderboards), and absence of hierarchy/guardrails, but is the closest ML-venue precedent for "paired + sequential + clustered".
- **Miller 2024**: paired differences, clustered SEs, power analysis for evals — fixed-horizon and scalar, but reviewers will ask what is new beyond "apply a CS instead of the CLT".
- **HAL / Kapoor et al.**: cost-controlled evaluation with Pareto frontiers and traces — the incumbent multi-objective summary.
- **Rabanser et al. ICML 2026; Mehta CLEAR**: multi-dimensional agent evaluation frameworks including safety and cost; they stop short of a decision statistic.
- **Ameli et al. ICLR 2025; WildBench**: ties modelled or thresholded inside pairwise LLM evaluation.
- **Adobe WWW'23; Karampatziakis ICML 2021**: anytime-valid gated deployment / A/B in production.
- **Skalse IJCAI 2022; Tercan ECAI 2024; Shakerinava 2025**: lexicographic and thresholded-lexicographic objectives, with axioms for non-scalarizability.
- **Clinical AI applications** (Real-POCQi, MAPS-LLM, GAPS-Agent; see `venue_and_novelty.md`): hierarchical/win comparisons of AI systems exist outside ML venues.

## 11. Opportunities credibly closable in 8 days

1. **Reanalysis showing rank reversals**: on τ² (4 trials × 3 domains × 3–4 models) and SWE-bench Lite (2 runs), compute success-only, cost-of-pass (Efficient Agents), Pareto membership, task-weighted GPC net benefit, and the guarded hierarchical decision; report task-clustered uncertainty (Miller-style paired SE, Bowyer-style small-n caveat, and our CS). A disagreement table is the "surprising fact" ICLR reviewers reward.
2. **Tie-threshold sensitivity grid** framed with WildBench's length-tie rule and Tercan's thresholded lexicographic order as precedents.
3. **Separability/resolution reporting** in Arena-Hard / Kotawala terms: fraction of system pairs resolved by n, and the sample size at which each pair's hierarchical decision stabilizes.
4. **Measurement-noise robustness**: use verifier tiers for success, measured cost, and a judged policy tier only as sensitivity, citing Messing/Landesberg/Kirgis for why.
5. **Position the online contribution** as identification (what pair-randomized A/B identifies) plus finite-sample guarded CS, explicitly citing Adobe/Karampatziakis for the pattern and Kotawala/CELEUS for contemporaneous anytime-valid LLM evaluation.
6. **Do not claim**: first sequential win statistic, first pairwise LLM evaluation with CIs, first anytime-valid LLM evaluation, first cost-aware agent evaluation, or first use of ties/thresholds in LLM pairwise comparison.
