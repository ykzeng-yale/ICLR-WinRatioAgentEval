# Industry practice: how AI agents are evaluated and A/B tested today

Audit date: 2026-09-17 (America/New_York). Purpose: ground the paper's framing in what agent-evaluation platforms and experimentation platforms actually do, so the hierarchical win-statistics + anytime-valid guardrail framework is positioned against real practice rather than a straw man. This file extends, and does not repeat, `evidence/venue_and_novelty.md` (academic prior art), `evidence/theory_design.md` (results and proof audit) and `evidence/empirical_feasibility.md` (data sources).

Every claim below is tied to a page that was actually opened during this audit (fetch date 2026-09-17). Items marked **UNVERIFIED** could not be opened (HTTP 403 or blocked archive) and must not be cited as if read. BibTeX keys refer to `paper/refs_industry.bib`.

## 0. Executive summary

1. Two largely separate tool stacks exist. **LLM/agent evaluation platforms** (LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize, Langfuse, W&B Weave, Humanloop, Confident AI/DeepEval, Galileo, Patronus, OpenAI Evals, Google's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform evals, Databricks/MLflow, Datadog Agent Observability) score traces with code graders, LLM judges and humans, offline on datasets and "online" on sampled production traces. They report per-metric averages, deltas versus a baseline, and sometimes raw pairwise win/loss counts; **none of the platform documentation opened here describes a valid stopping rule, a multiplicity rule, or a guardrail rule for deciding between two agent versions**. **Experimentation platforms** (Statsig, Eppo/Datadog Experiments, Optimizely, GrowthBook, LaunchDarkly, Spotify Confidence, Microsoft ExP, Netflix) have exactly those rules: primary/secondary/guardrail metric tiers, sequential or always-valid tests, non-inferiority margins, FDR control, automatic rollback. But they treat each metric as a separate scalar test and resolve conflicts between metrics informally ("no standard way", ExP) or by a conjunction of per-metric tests (Spotify).
2. The metric vocabulary used for agents in production is stable across vendors: (i) a task/outcome success signal (state check, rubric, LLM judge, human, customer confirmation), (ii) trajectory/tool-use quality (tool selection, argument correctness, tool errors, step efficiency), (iii) safety/policy compliance, (iv) operational metrics (latency, tokens, cost per task, error rate), (v) reliability across repeated trials (pass^k), and (vi) business/user outcomes (resolution rate, escalation/handoff, CSAT, reopen rate, retention). These are heterogeneous in type, priority, and time-to-observation, which is exactly the setting for prioritized (hierarchical) pairwise comparison.
3. The convergence point is recent and explicit: Spotify (May 2026) frames LLM evals as a funnel feeding A/B tests and reports rolling back 42% of launched experiments because secondary metrics regressed; Datadog Experiments (April 2026, from the Eppo acquisition) markets "real-time observability guardrails" for validating AI-driven changes; LaunchDarkly AgentControl couples guarded rollouts with online LLM judges; Sierra (June 2026) describes A/B testing agent behavior changes on a slice of traffic until statistical significance. The paper can credibly say: practitioners already run continuous, guardrailed comparisons of agent versions, with scalar-metric tooling not designed for prioritized, heterogeneous, paired agent outcomes.
4. Pairwise (win/tie/loss) judgments are already the native output of LangSmith and Braintrust pairwise evaluators, of Arena-style leaderboards (Bradley-Terry), and of interleaving experiments at Netflix/Airbnb, whose preference statistic is literally a net-benefit-type "wins minus losses over N". What is missing in all of them is (a) a priority hierarchy over multiple outcome components with meaningful ties, (b) anytime-valid inference for the resulting pairwise statistic, and (c) a coupling of that statistic to population-level guardrails.

Observations that matter for the paper:

- Vendors already separate "operational telemetry" (E-G) from "evaluation scores" (A-C) explicitly (`datadog2026metrics`: evaluation scores are "not derived from operational telemetry"). A hierarchy that places A/B-family outcomes above E/F-family resource use mirrors this separation rather than inventing it.
- Composite scalarization is the only cross-metric aggregation offered by any tool: OpenAI multigrader `calculate_output` formulas (`openai2026graders`), CLEAR's weighted composite with customizable weights (`mehta2025clear`), and Microsoft ExP's suggestion of "pre-determined weighted metrics" (`machmouchi2021post`). No opened source aggregates heterogeneous metrics by priority order with tie thresholds, which is the GPC/win-ratio idea.
- Cost-accuracy Pareto reporting (HAL, `kapoor2025hal`) is the academic alternative; it describes but does not decide.

### 3.2 Pain points with evidence

1. **Peeking and continuous monitoring.** Statsig: continuous monitoring "inflates the false positive rate"; Spotify: "a main objective of experimentation is to know early on if end users are negatively affected"; ExP requires accounting for "multiple hypothesis testing and early peeking"; LaunchDarkly checks "multiple times per minute". Sequential/always-valid methods are now standard in experimentation platforms but absent from every LLM-eval platform opened (LangSmith, Braintrust, Langfuse, Weave, Humanloop, Galileo, OpenAI, Google, Statsig AI Evals).
2. **Multiple heterogeneous metrics and conflicting movements.** ExP: "Is the improvement in the OEC enough to justify the regression in the Guardrail metric?" with "no standard way" to answer; Statsig: "an early significant result on some metrics doesn't guarantee enough power to detect regressions in other metrics"; Spotify: "with 5 guardrails powered at 80%, simultaneous power drops below 40%" and 42% of launched experiments rolled back for secondary-metric regressions; Optimizely dilutes monitoring metrics by 1/n; GrowthBook: "Choosing too many guardrail metrics increases the chance of false positives." Agent evaluations routinely carry 9-20+ metrics (Galileo nine agentic metrics; Google 20+; Datadog separate evaluation and telemetry families).
3. **Guardrails as afterthoughts to a scalar primary.** Eppo/GrowthBook/LaunchDarkly implement guardrails as one-sided CIs on each metric; the primary decision is a separate scalar test. No platform ties a guardrail to a *paired* preference statistic.
4. **Cost/latency trade-offs are decided by eye.** HAL: "less than one-third of tested models are on the frontier" and token prices differ by two orders of magnitude; Datadog's own AI Guard evaluation compares accuracy/F1 with "latency and token cost per evaluation" as separate columns; CLEAR resorts to customizable weights; Anthropic lists latency/tokens/cost/error rates to track but gives no combination rule.
5. **Delayed and sparse outcomes.** Resolution confirmed via reopen windows of 24-48 h; CSAT response rates 2-8% (`fin2026resolution`); Spotify's "peeking problem 2.0": within-unit peeking on outcomes that mature over time inflates FPR; Netflix uses 14-day surrogate indices to approximate day-63 decisions (~95% consistent). Agent A/B tests thus mix immediately observed components (state checks, latency, cost) with slowly maturing ones (resolution, reopen, retention).
6. **Heterogeneous tasks and paired designs.** Anthropic: clustered SEs "over three times as large as naive"; paired differences recommended because models "get the same questions right and wrong". LangSmith and Braintrust pairwise evaluators and Statsig's shadow candidates are paired by construction; production A/B randomization (Sierra, LaunchDarkly, Langfuse) is not.
7. **Noisy graders and ties.** LangChain: absolute criteria gave "a perfect score" to 3 of 4 models; Galileo's TSQ needs a majority of several judge calls; Arena supports ties and "both are bad". Pairwise judgments with explicit ties are already the practical answer, but their aggregation is a raw percentage without inference.
8. **Reliability vs capability.** pass^k drops (GPT-4o "~25% on pass^8"); regression suites should be at "nearly 100%"; CLEAR reports "60% (single run) to 25% (8-run consistency)". Repeated trials are needed, but they multiply comparisons within task and inflate naive sample sizes.
9. **Offline-online gap.** Spotify: "Without offline-online signal calibration, our evals are opinions, not evidence"; only 12% of A/B tests ship positive; DoorDash validates with simulators before deployment; Airbnb interleaving agrees directionally with A/B only 82% of the time.

## 4. What a hierarchical win-statistics + anytime-valid guardrail framework addresses, and what it does not

| Pain point | Addressed by the proposed framework? | Mechanism and caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Peeking / continuous monitoring | Yes | Confidence sequences for the pairwise hierarchical score and one-sided guardrail sequences; same family as Eppo (Howard et al.) and GrowthBook (asymptotic CS), so practitioners already accept the tool. Caveat: must be compared to GST/mSPRT baselines on power (Spotify chose GST for that reason). |
| Multiple heterogeneous metrics | Yes, for the *preference* decision | A single prespecified priority order with tie thresholds replaces ad hoc weight formulas (`openai2026graders`, `mehta2025clear`) and the "no standard way" trade-off (`machmouchi2021post`). Caveat: the hierarchy is a normative input, and the population-priority obstruction in `theory_design.md` shows the pairwise winner can still be worse on the marginal primary rate; hence guardrails remain necessary. |
| Guardrails coupled to the decision | Yes | Deployment = pairwise CS excludes zero (or margin) AND every population guardrail CS clears its non-inferiority margin; intersection-union logic already matches Spotify's ship rule and Eppo's cutoff semantics, now for a paired statistic. Caveat: power loss with many guardrails (Spotify's β/(G+1)) applies equally; do not claim to remove it. |
| Cost/latency trade-offs | Partly | Cost/latency enter as lower tiers with practical-equivalence thresholds (Statsig's "how bad is bad enough"), yielding a decision instead of a Pareto plot. Caveat: the sensitivity of the decision to thresholds must be reported. |
| Delayed outcomes | Partly | Enrollment-prefix display (theory item 9) keeps validity when some components mature later; explicit missingness protocol needed. Caveat: Spotify's "peeking 2.0" shows the estimand itself changes when within-unit outcomes mature; the framework does not solve surrogate selection. |
| Heterogeneous tasks / pairing | Yes, when pairing is available | Task-paired offline replays and shadow/candidate designs (Statsig AI Evals, LangSmith/Braintrust pairwise) identify same-input preference; single-exposure A/B identifies only the cross-arrival contrast (`theory_design.md`). This distinction is not made in any vendor doc and is a contribution to practice. |
| Noisy graders and ties | Yes | Ties are first-class (meaningful-difference thresholds), matching the native 0/1/2 and win/tie/loss outputs of LangSmith, Braintrust and Arena. Caveat: judge bias (position, verbosity) is orthogonal and must be handled by randomized order as LangSmith does. |
| Reliability / repeated trials | Partly | Task is the independent unit; within-task replicates are pooled into a task-level statistic (theory item 11). pass^k itself is not the target. |
| Offline-online gap | No | The framework does not calibrate evals to user outcomes; it can only run the same decision rule in both regimes. |
| Sample-ratio mismatch, data-quality tests | No | Out of scope; recommend inheriting platform quality tests (Spotify "quality metrics", ExP SRM alerts). |

## 5. Motivating-example material for the introduction (with citations)

Candidate opening vignette (all numbers are from the cited sources; wording is ours):

- A team ships a new coding-agent scaffold. The regression suite should stay at "nearly 100%" while capability evals hill-climb (`anthropic2026demystifying`). Their dashboards report success, `n_toolcalls`, `n_total_tokens`, latency percentiles (`anthropic2026demystifying`, `otel2026genaimetrics`) and LLM-judge scores for tool selection and action completion (`galileo2025agentic`). Version B raises pass@1 by a little but doubles tool calls and cost; the cost-accuracy frontier contains "less than one-third of tested models" and token prices differ by "two orders of magnitude" (`kapoor2025hal`). Nothing in the stack decides.
- Experimentation platforms would decide, but scalar by scalar: Spotify ships only if a success metric is superior and all guardrails are non-inferior, and reports that 42% of launches were later rolled back for secondary-metric regressions and that five guardrails cut simultaneous power below 40% (`spotify2026llmevals`, `spotify2024riskaware`). Microsoft ExP concedes there is "no standard way" to weigh an OEC gain against a guardrail loss (`machmouchi2021post`).
- Pairwise judgments already exist in the tooling: LangSmith and Braintrust record per-input wins, ties and losses and show "how often the base experiment was preferred, as a percent" with no confidence statement (`langsmith2026pairwise`, `braintrust2026compare`); Arena aggregates pairwise votes by Bradley-Terry with intervals (`chiang2024chatbotarena`); Airbnb's interleaving statistic is the mean of sign(wins − losses) per user, which is a net benefit, and it gave "about 50X speedup from A/B" (`zhang2025interleaving`).
- Online, teams peek continuously: LaunchDarkly checks guarded rollouts "multiple times per minute" (`launchdarkly2026regression`); Sierra ramps agent variants "as confidence grows" and ships at significance (`sierra2026customers`); GrowthBook's safe rollouts fail a guardrail at any statistically certain harm, "even by very small amounts" (`growthbook2026saferollouts`). Resolution outcomes mature over 24-48 h reopen windows and CSAT responses arrive from 2-8% of conversations (`fin2026resolution`).
- The gap: no opened tool combines (i) prioritized pairwise comparison of heterogeneous, tie-prone agent outcomes, (ii) anytime-valid inference for that comparison, and (iii) population-level non-inferiority guardrails in one deployment rule. Practitioners already accept each ingredient separately (Eppo and GrowthBook use confidence sequences; Spotify uses non-inferiority guardrails; LangSmith/Braintrust use pairwise judgments).

Suggested one-sentence framing: "Agent teams already collect prioritized, heterogeneous, paired outcomes and already monitor them continuously; the missing piece is a decision rule whose error guarantees survive both the prioritization and the monitoring."

## 6. Practical design implications for the paper's experiments

1. Use the vendor metric names when defining tiers (task success from state check; policy violation; tool-call count; wall-clock; cost in USD) so the hierarchy reads as a configuration of existing telemetry (`otel2026genaimetrics`, `datadog2026metrics`).
2. Report the naive alternatives practitioners would actually run: raw pairwise win percentage (Braintrust), per-metric mSPRT/CS with a fixed guardrail margin (Statsig/Eppo/GrowthBook), Spotify's conjunction rule with β/(G+1) power correction, and a weighted composite (`openai2026graders`, `mehta2025clear`).
3. Include a "shadow-candidate" design (Statsig AI Evals; Microsoft's shadow tests) as the paired online regime, and a single-exposure randomized rollout (LaunchDarkly/Sierra) as the unpaired regime; state explicitly which estimand each identifies.
4. Model delayed components with an enrollment-prefix display and cite Spotify's within-unit peeking problem as the reason the estimand must be fixed before monitoring.
5. Do not claim to solve judge bias, offline-online calibration, or surrogate selection; cite `spotify2026llmevals` and `zhang2023surrogate` as the boundaries.
