# Literature sweep: win statistics and generalized pairwise comparisons

Sweep date: 2026-09-17 (US Eastern). Author: research subagent. This file extends `evidence/venue_and_novelty.md` and `evidence/theory_design.md`; it does not repeat their confidence-sequence / OPE audit. Every row below was checked against a source that was actually opened in this pass (publisher abstract page, Europe PMC record, arXiv abstract or arXiv HTML full text). Where only an abstract or a record was opened, the "Inspected" column says so; theorem-level statements are given only for sources whose full text (or a structured publisher summary of the methods) was read. Anything not opened is labelled UNVERIFIED. BibTeX for every verified source is in `paper/refs_winstats.bib`.

Notation used throughout: for one unit from arm A and one from arm B, let `p_w = P(A wins)`, `p_l = P(B wins)`, `p_t = 1 - p_w - p_l` (tie). Then

- net benefit / win difference / "proportion in favour": `NB = p_w - p_l` (Buyse 2010; called win difference by Pocock et al. 2024 and Fang et al. 2026);
- win ratio: `WR = p_w / p_l` (Pocock et al. 2012);
- win odds: `WO = (p_w + p_t/2) / (p_l + p_t/2)` (Dong et al. 2020; Brunner et al. 2021), and algebraically `WO = (1 + NB)/(1 - NB)`;
- DOOR probability: `p_w + p_t/2` (Evans and Follmann 2016; Fang et al. 2026).

## 3. Theorem-level notes

### 3.1 Estimands and identification (Mao 2018; Even and Josse 2026; Lee et al. 2026; Dong et al. 2026)

- Mao (2018), Biometrika 105(1):215-220. For a contrast kernel h, the causal estimand is `tau_h = E[h(Y_1(1), Y_2(0))]` over two *independent* replicates, equivalently the bilinear functional of the two marginal potential-outcome laws. The individual contrast `tau*_h = E[h(Y(1), Y(0))]` is not identifiable because the joint law of (Y(1),Y(0)) is not. Under unconfoundedness tau_h is identified; the IPW U-statistic is consistent and asymptotically linear; Theorem 1 characterizes influence functions of regular estimators; Theorem 2 gives a locally efficient doubly robust estimator. Win ratio with ordinal outcomes is a named application.
  - For us: with h the antisymmetric hierarchical score and A/B randomization, our cross-arrival online estimand is tau_h. Our Theorem 3 (cyclic couplings with the same marginals) is a concrete instance of Mao's non-identifiability remark; present it as illustration, cite Mao.
- Even and Josse (v4). Definition 1: win function w(y | y') in {1, 1/2, 0} (or discard ties). Section 2.3, eq. (10): tau_indiv = E[w(Y_i(1) | Y_i(0))], non-identifiable (footnote Bernoulli example). Eq. (11): tau_pop = E[w(Y_i(1) | Y_j(0))], i != j. Definition 2, eqs. (12)-(15): tau_*(x) = E[w(Y^{(x)}(1) | Y_i(0)) | X_i = x] with Y^{(x)} an independent copy at the same covariates; tau_* = E[tau_*(X_i)], collapsible by construction. Identification (eq. 16) under Assumptions 1-3 (SUTVA, unconfoundedness, positivity eta <= pi(x) <= 1 - eta). Theorem 1 (RCT, T independent of X): complete pairing estimator -> tau_pop; nearest-neighbour matched estimator (eq. 18) -> tau_* under compact X and continuity of x -> E[w(Y(1) | y) | X = x]; Theorem 2 (observational) under unconfoundedness. Proposition 1: |tau_* - tau_indiv| <= d_TV between the law of (Y^{(X)}(1), Y(0)) and the law of (Y(1), Y(0)). Example 2 (Hand's paradox): with subpopulation structure and harm fraction alpha in (1 - 1/sqrt 2, 1/2), tau_* > 1/2 > tau_pop = (1 - alpha)^2, so the two targets can recommend opposite decisions. Sections 4.1-4.2: distributional-regression plug-in tau = (1/n) sum p_1(X_i) with p_1(x) = E[w(Y_i|Y_j) | X_i = X_j = x, T_i = 1, T_j = 0], and a one-step EIF-debiased estimator attaining root-n rates.
  - For us: with X = task identifier (finite), tau_*(x) is the same-task independent-replicate win probability we call the offline target, and exact matching is available, so NN consistency conditions are trivially met. The online paired design targets tau_pop within a stratum; the Hand's-paradox example shows why offline and online rankings can legitimately disagree. Cite as the direct predecessor for the marginal-vs-conditional distinction (already flagged in venue_and_novelty.md).
- Lee, Fang, Li, Harhay (2026). Individual-pair vs cluster-pair win estimands differ, possibly in sign, under informative cluster size; cluster-pair estimator is unbiased in finite samples, individual-pair is not; leave-one-cluster-out jackknife. For us: "pool all runs" vs "average over tasks" are different estimands when run counts differ by task; we already fix task-weighted targets, cite this for the terminology.
- Dong et al. (2026) noncollapsibility: WR is noncollapsible for every outcome type; NB and WO are collapsible only for binary outcomes. For us: a prespecified-weight combination of stratum NBs (our Theorem 10) is a standardized estimand and should be named as such; it equals the marginal NB only under special conditions.

### 3.2 Fixed-sample inference for two-sample win statistics (Bebu and Lachin 2016; Luo et al. 2015; Dong et al. 2016; Brunner et al. 2021; Dong et al. 2023)

- All are two-sample U-statistic results: with n_1 treated and n_0 controls, `U_w = (1/(n_1 n_0)) sum_i sum_j I[i wins over j]`, `U_l` analogously. Joint asymptotic normality of (U_w, U_l) with covariance from the Hoeffding/Hajek projections (Bebu and Lachin; Luo et al. for a specific two-component rule; Dong et al. 2016 for an arbitrary rule). Log WR by delta method; Fieller intervals as alternative. Stratified versions pool U-statistics across a fixed number of strata (Bebu and Lachin) or use MH-type weights (Dong et al. 2018).
- Brunner et al. (2021): the WR inference ignores ties; the WO incorporates them via the Mann-Whitney relative effect p = P(X < Y) + P(X = Y)/2, with rank-based variance estimation; recommends WO.
- Dong et al. (2023, pst.2251) and Dong et al. (2023, pst.2293): WR, WO, NB test the same H0 (p_w = p_l), their Z-values are approximately equal, and this persists under stratification.
- For us: the offline fixed-horizon comparator should be the Bebu-Lachin/Dong-2016 all-pairs-within-task statistic aggregated at task level (see 3.4), with the task as the independent unit. Because agent outcomes have many exact ties (binary success, equal cost bins), report NB (and DOOR probability) rather than WR; WR is undefined or unstable when p_l is near 0 (our Theorem 11 already notes ratio uncertainty is bounded only when p_l is bounded away from 0).

### 3.3 Estimand dependence on the observation window (Oakes 2016; Mao 2024)

- Oakes: with follow-up truncated at c, win and loss probabilities are integrals over the shared window; the WR generally depends on the distribution of potential follow-up times; it is c-free only under bivariate Lehmann (proportional hazards) models.
- Mao 2024: the WR estimand is ill-defined unless the comparison time frame is prespecified; otherwise it is set by trial-specific censoring.
- For us: latency timeouts and evaluation horizons play the role of c. The estimand must state the timeout/horizon; changing the horizon changes the target. This is consistent with our delay result (display only fully observed prefixes) and rules out discarding timeouts.

### 3.4 Clustered inference (Fang et al. 2026)

- Setup: M independent clusters, arbitrary within-cluster dependence, non-informative cluster size, proportion q of clusters treated. Cluster score S_i = sum over pairs involving cluster i of s in {-1,0,1}. Clustered rank-sum (FCL) Wald variance (their eq. 5): `sigma^2_WD = (M q (1-q)/(n_1 n_0))^2 { s_1^2/(qM) + s_0^2/((1-q)M) }` with within-arm cluster-score variances, delta method for WR/WO/DOOR. Bivariate clustered U-statistic (ZJ) covariance (eq. 6) `Sigma_U = (Nbar_0^2/M_1) Sigma_1 + (Nbar_1^2/M_0) Sigma_0`. Delete-one-cluster jackknife `(M-1)/M sum_i (tau_(-i) - taubar)^2`. Permutation test over (M choose qM) assignments (sharp null); cluster Finkelstein-Schoenfeld randomization variance (eq. 7) `q(1-q) M/(M-1) sum_i S_i^2` (weak null). Jackknife empirical likelihood with pseudo-values, chi-square_1 limit. Simulations: at M = 20, permutation and cluster-FS control size, jackknife/ZJ inflate, FCL conservative; at M = 100 all agree.
- For us: in the offline benchmark, tasks are the clusters, and within-task cross-comparisons of replicate runs are the within-cluster pair scores. Our task-level statistic and CLT (theory item 11) are the FCL/cluster-score construction specialized to a paired (every task sees both arms) design; cite Fang et al. and note the paired structure removes the between-arm cluster-assignment randomness.

### 3.5 Sequential and group-sequential win statistics (Zhang and Wu 2024; Bergemann and Hanson 2026; Huang et al. 2024)

- Zhang and Wu: interim statistic at stage k is the cumulative all-pairs U-statistic over m_k treated and n_k controls enrolled so far (eq. 3.1). Theorem 3.4: the vector of standardized sequential win/loss statistics across K looks is asymptotically multivariate normal with the canonical (independent-increments) covariance; Proposition 3.1 gives `Cov(D_p, D_q) = sqrt(V(Delta U_q)/V(Delta U_p))` for p < q with information equal to the variance of NB (or of log WR). Condition C1: arm and stage sample fractions converge. Assumes independent subjects and censoring independent of outcomes with common censoring across endpoints. Variance components xi_10, xi_01, xi_11 estimated empirically (Section 9.2). Alpha spending alpha(t_k) = alpha t_k^2. Algorithm 1 searches N by simulation. K = 3 simulations: type I error controlled, ASN smaller than fixed design (e.g., 352 vs 432 at 90% power).
- Bergemann and Hanson: same all-pairs U-statistic; Proposition 1 (win difference) and Proposition 2 (log WR, Taylor) establish independent increments; requires a fixed evaluation time T with complete follow-up for subjects included at earlier looks; information fraction = fraction of subjects (or person-years); HSD spending gamma = -3 via gsDesign; type I error 0.0517/0.0487 (complete) and 0.0444/0.0414 (partial) at N = 200/400 over 10,000 simulations.
- Huang, Wang, Ning: Bayesian futility monitoring with WR over prioritized time-to-event endpoints.
- For us: three sequential precedents, all asymptotic and planned-look (or Bayesian), using the all-pairs statistic. Our CS uses disjoint online pairs with finite-sample validity at every time and under adaptive orientation. The price is efficiency: for n units per arm the all-pairs U-statistic has asymptotic variance of order (zeta_10 + zeta_01)/n, whereas n/2 disjoint pairs give Var(h)/(n/2); the ratio depends on how much of Var(h) is explained by the first-order projections. This must be computed and reported for our score distributions rather than asserted.

### 3.6 Anytime-valid U-statistic inference (Cai, Hu, Li 2026)

- Setting: iid X_i, symmetric degree-2 kernel h with E h^2 < infinity, theta = E h(X_1, X_2), Hoeffding decomposition U_n - theta = (2/n) sum h_1(X_i) + R_n. Definition 1: asymptotic CS (AsympCS) in the sense of Waudby-Smith et al. Theorem 1: under E|h|^{2+delta} < infinity, strong Gaussian approximation with a.s. error O(n^{-1+1/(2+delta)}). Theorem 2: leave-one-out jackknife variance estimator (eq. 6) with a.s. error O(sqrt(log log n/n)) under E|h|^4 < infinity. Theorem 3: [U_n +/- 2 sigma_hat_n gamma_{alpha,m}(n)] is a (1 - alpha)-AsympCS with width sqrt(log log n/n) (stitched LIL boundary) or sqrt(log n/n) (Gaussian mixture). Degenerate case (h_1 = 0): Theorem 4 Gaussian-chaos approximation; Theorem 5 SAGE boundary; Theorem 6 plug-in spectral version; width log log n/n. Examples: sample variance, Gini mean difference, spatial Kendall tau, MMD. Section 6 lists dependent data, vector-valued and higher-degree kernels as open.
- For us: this is one-sample, iid, asymptotic. Our normal-mixture CS is finite-sample, uses disjoint pairs, and has width sqrt(log n/n) (not LIL-rate). A reviewer comparison should state (i) finite-sample vs asymptotic validity, (ii) iid vs adaptive/drifting pair sequence, (iii) efficiency loss from disjoint pairs, (iv) that a two-sample time-uniform U-statistic CS is not stated in Cai et al. but is a plausible corollary a reviewer may expect us to acknowledge.

### 3.7 Paradoxes, tier filtering, and hierarchy sensitivity (Fu 2026; Verbeeck et al. 2019; Li, Fan, Yang 2026; McCoy et al. 2026)

- Fu (2026): two binary endpoints, exact 2x2 table; treatment has higher marginal success probability on both, yet WR < 1 and NB < 0, because the secondary endpoint is compared only within primary-tie strata, which reweights toward strata where treatment does worse; decomposition of NB into tie-stratum contributions; proposes reporting diagnostics.
- Verbeeck et al. (2019): prioritized GPC methods "do not measure the true treatment effect from the second priority-ranked component onward"; sensitive to priority order.
- Li, Fan, Yang (2026): estimand = min over protocol-consistent rules r of NB_r; each NB_r a two-sample U-statistic; intersection-union test inverted to a one-sided lower confidence bound with no multiplicity adjustment.
- McCoy et al. (2026): PSNB = sum over stages of prespecified priority weights times stage-specific NB, replacing data-driven reach-probability weights.
- For us: our population-priority obstruction (Theorem 2) and Fu's paradox are two faces of tie-stratum reweighting; cite Fu and Verbeeck. Our comparator-sensitivity bound |theta_h - theta_g| <= E|h - g| (Theorem 13) and the "prespecified families need simultaneous inference" remark are close to Li et al.; the anytime-valid version (a CS for the minimum NB over a finite rule family via union/IUT) is a small but clean extension we can state and prove in a paragraph. PSNB is a comparator to our guardrail-plus-hierarchy protocol and should appear in the sensitivity grid.

### 3.8 Matched-pair win statistics (Pocock et al. 2012; Matsouaka 2022)

- Pocock's matched version pairs by risk score and treats N_w/(N_w + N_l) as a binomial proportion. Matsouaka (2022) gives for paired designs a null test statistic, sample size, and MOVER confidence intervals for NB, WR, WO that respect the [-1,1] and (0, infinity) boundaries and outperform Wald intervals when proportions are near 0 or 1 or n is small.
- For us: our online design is a matched-pair design (disjoint pairs, randomized AB/BA); the fixed-sample comparator is Matsouaka's, and our CS is its sequential counterpart. Boundary behaviour (p_l near 0) is exactly where our ratio-uncertainty caveat bites.

### 3.9 DOOR/RADAR (Evans et al. 2015; Evans and Follmann 2016)

- DOOR: ordinal overall-outcome rank per patient; RADAR breaks ties in DOOR by shorter antibiotic duration, with the explicit rule that a worse clinical outcome can never outrank a better one. Estimand: P(new better) + 0.5 P(tie) = p_w + p_t/2. Inference: Mann-Whitney; bootstrap CIs; partial-credit sensitivity over a range of level weights.
- For us: RADAR is the precedent for "quality tier first, resource tier as tie-breaker". The partial-credit sweep is a precedent for our priority/threshold sensitivity grid.

## 4. What the sweep implies for method design

1. **Name the estimands with the literature's names.** Online paired A/B: Mao's tau_h / Even-Josse tau_pop within stratum (population pairwise NB). Offline same-task replicates: Even-Josse tau_*(x) with x = task, aggregated by prespecified task weights (a standardized, collapsible target; Dong et al. 2026 warn WR is noncollapsible). All-runs pooled vs task-averaged: Lee et al.'s individual-pair vs cluster-pair distinction.
2. **Prefer NB and DOOR probability; report WR only descriptively.** Ties are frequent in agent outcomes; Brunner 2021, Ajufo 2023 and Dong 2023 all argue WR ignores ties; ratio inference degenerates when p_l is near 0 (Matsouaka 2022; our Theorem 11).
3. **Fixed-horizon comparators to include.** Offline: Bebu-Lachin/Dong-2016 U-statistic with task clustering (Fang et al. cluster-score variance, or leave-one-task-out jackknife). Online paired: Matsouaka MOVER intervals. Sequential: Zhang-Wu / Bergemann-Hanson alpha-spending on the all-pairs statistic, and Cai et al.'s AsympCS with jackknife variance as the asymptotic anytime baseline. Report efficiency (interval width at matched n) and validity (coverage at small n, under adaptive orientation, under drift).
4. **Tie-filtering paradoxes are known; the remedy and its evidence are ours to supply.** Cite Fu 2026 and Verbeeck 2019 for the phenomenon; position guardrails (population-level noninferiority gates) against PSNB (McCoy) and worst-case NB over rule families (Li-Fan-Yang) as alternative remedies, and show on tau2-bench / SWE-bench which decisions differ.
5. **Hierarchy sensitivity as a simultaneous target.** A CS for min over a finite prespecified rule family is immediate from the union bound (or from our IUT-style conjunction). Stating it connects our Theorem 13 to Li-Fan-Yang and gives a deployable "robust to unresolved tier order" gate.
6. **Observation window is part of the estimand** (Oakes; Mao 2024). Timeouts must be outcomes, and the horizon must be prespecified; this supports our delay theorem's refusal to discard timeouts.
7. **The nearest ML precedents are Huang (arXiv:2609.07785) and Kotawala (arXiv:2605.30315).** Huang uses the same tau2-bench data, paired bootstrap, a 2-pp margin, and a *linear* cost-utility tradeoff with a switching lambda; Kotawala uses an anytime-valid paired e-process on binary accuracy. Our paper must show what the hierarchical score plus guardrails plus finite-sample CS adds over (a) linear scalarization with bootstrap and (b) anytime-valid accuracy-only comparison.
